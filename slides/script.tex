% ================================================================ 1
\slide{1}{Back to entropy}{0:00--0:15}
\begin{say}
This talk is about one question: which base model will fine-tune best on our data, and how to
know before we fine-tune anything.
\end{say}

% ================================================================ 2
\slide{2}{Which base model will fine-tune best on our data?}{0:15--1:00}
\begin{say}
Fine-tuning every candidate would settle it, but that needs a task, labels and a training run per
model. So we read a scorecard instead: MMLU-Pro, GSM8K, HellaSwag. Those grade a model before the
fine-tuning it will get. Look at these two small models. Two points apart on HellaSwag, two percent
apart on our news text, fifty-five points apart on GSM8K. Fine-tuned on news, they cannot be told
apart.
\end{say}

% ================================================================ 3
\slide{3}{Read the domain's documents; the fine-tuned order follows}{1:00--1:55}
\begin{say}
Here is the result. Fifteen base models, each fine-tuned on the same news-writing task. Each line
joins a model's rank before fine-tuning to its rank after. On GSM8K and MMLU-Pro the lines cross
everywhere. On the right we rank by bits per byte on whole news documents, with no training at
all, and the lines run almost flat: every model lands within one rank of where it finishes. The
blue line is Gemma-4-12B, ninth on GSM8K, and it builds the best fine-tuned system of the fifteen.
\end{say}
\cue{LAND THIS: one forward pass, and the fine-tuned order is already there.}

% ================================================================ 4
\slide{4}{Pair by pair}{1:55--2:40}
\begin{say}
The fair test is pairs of models of similar size, because between very different sizes the bigger
one usually wins. There are thirty-nine such pairs. Document bits per byte orders thirty-seven of
them the way the fine-tune does. HellaSwag thirty-four, size twenty-eight, and GSM8K and MMLU-Pro
are a coin flip. So is each model's own score on the task before any training: fine-tuning
reorders the models, and the document reading predicts the new order. A second, very different fine-tuning recipe gives the same picture.
\end{say}

% ================================================================ 5
\slide{5}{Why this is not already how it is done}{2:40--3:25}
\begin{say}
If this sounds obvious, the literature says the opposite. The closest test we know of fine-tuned
fifty one-billion models and found that perplexity ordered them worse than chance. Two details
explain the difference, and the usual practice gets both wrong: which text you score, and how you
cut it.
\end{say}

% ================================================================ 6
\slide{6}{Detail 1: blocks cut from a stream misrank strong models}{3:25--4:15}
\begin{say}
The common way to compute perplexity joins the test set into one stream and cuts it into blocks.
Most blocks then start in the middle of a document, with no start token. Some models pay heavily
for that. Gemma opens each block far worse than a model eight times smaller and has passed it by
the end. On blocks Gemma comes thirteenth of fifteen; on whole documents, second; fine-tuned,
first.
\end{say}

% ================================================================ 7
\slide{7}{Detail 2: the text has to be like the task's}{4:15--5:05}
\begin{say}
Second, the text. Score the same models on arXiv mathematics and use that to predict the news
fine-tune, and you get twenty-three of thirty-nine pairs, worse than ranking by size. And which
benchmark the ranking agrees with follows the text: on news, Reddit and Hacker News it is
HellaSwag; on mathematics it is GSM8K and MMLU-Pro. We wrote that rule down before reading the new
corpora.
\end{say}

% ================================================================ 8
\slide{8}{No training, and no noise}{5:05--5:50}
\begin{say}
We started by adapting every model briefly to the domain first, and on blocks that is a repair. On
whole documents it lowers every model's loss but barely changes the order: one pair in thirty-nine.
So no training is needed. And the reading is deterministic: run it again and you get the same
number to the last digit, where the same weights on a new machine move GSM8K by more than half a
point.
\end{say}

% ================================================================ 9
\slide{9}{Why loss is the right quantity}{5:50--6:30}
\begin{say}
Why should this work? A base model learns everything it knows by minimising this loss, and
fine-tuning minimises it again on the task. The loss splits into the entropy of the text, the same
for every model, plus the model's distance from the text. Per byte, every tokenizer is on one
scale, and on fresh text no model has trained on it.
\end{say}

% ================================================================ 10
\slide{10}{Using it, and adding your model}{6:30--7:00}
\begin{say}
The pipeline reports this number for every candidate in one forward pass. The leaderboard now ranks
by it, on news and on a public maths corpus anyone can run.
\end{say}

\slide{11}{Score the candidates on your own documents}{7:00--7:08}
\begin{say}
So: score the candidates on your own documents. Thank you, happy to take questions.
\end{say}

{\large\bfseries Q\&A preparation}

\textbf{``Why not just fine-tune on the task?''} When you have the task, its labels and the budget,
do. A base model is usually chosen before any of those exist.

\textbf{``Does it beat HellaSwag?''} It has the top point estimate, 37 against 34 pairs, but with
fifteen models the intervals overlap. HellaSwag is close on news; on mathematics text the ranking
follows GSM8K and MMLU-Pro instead, so HellaSwag is not a general substitute.

\textbf{``Only one task?''} Yes, one fine-tuned task. We tried two on mathematics; on both a 0.5B and
a 12B model finished within 0.012 ROUGE-L, so the tasks could not rank models and we stopped them
by a rule fixed in advance.

\textbf{``What about contamination of our own text?''} Text dated after every release, deduplicated
with no split overlap, and membership screens with a quarantine of anything any model flags.

\textbf{``What does it cost?''} One forward pass over about six megabytes of text per model.

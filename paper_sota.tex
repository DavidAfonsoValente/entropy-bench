\begin{abstract}
Perplexity once anchored language-model evaluation in predictive cross-entropy, but its token
denominator prevents comparisons across tokenizers. Fixed task benchmarks provide common test
sets, yet each observes only selected capabilities and can be distorted by benchmark-specific
optimization or training-data overlap. We return base-model selection to the language-modeling
objective with three adjustments: normalize cross-entropy by UTF-8 bytes, evaluate dynamically on
fresh target-domain data before and after controlled adaptation, and filter contamination before
scoring. Across 11 base models from 0.5B to 35B,
adapted BPB tracks sentence-continuation quality closely ($r=-0.96$ against HellaSwag), while
knowledge and arithmetic benchmarks reveal complementary strengths. Token-level analysis gives the
reported quantities distinct roles: adapted BPB selects the model; zero-shot$\rightarrow$adapted
reduction describes corpus calibration. A fixed-target check shows that 512-token adapters retain
their gains through 2{,}048-token inference (a $9.6\%$ BPB reduction for Gemma-4-12B and $1.7\%$
for Qwen-2.5-1.5B). Under one shared budget on news, Reddit, and Hacker News, adaptation materially
reorders candidates within each corpus (zero-shot versus adapted Spearman $\rho=0.609$--$0.682$),
while the adapted ranking is highly stable across corpora ($\rho=+0.991$, $+0.955$, and $+0.964$).
The resulting rule is simple: rank models by adapted BPB on recent target-domain data and resolve
near-ties within a tier using the target domain.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Language models were originally judged by how well their predictive distributions compressed text:
cross-entropy and its exponentiated form, perplexity. As evaluation shifted toward fixed task suites,
scores became easier to communicate but narrower in scope. MMLU, HellaSwag, and GSM8K each expose a
useful slice of capability rather than the full quality of a base language model
\cite{hendrycks2021mmlu,zellers2019hellaswag,cobbe2021gsm8k}. Once a fixed benchmark also becomes a
development target, benchmark-specific optimization and training-data overlap further weaken its
value as an independent measure \cite{xu2024survey}.

We argue for going back to the language-modeling objective, but not to raw perplexity. Modern base
models span dense Transformers \cite{vaswani2017attention}, sparse Mixture-of-Experts (MoE), Liquid
Neural Networks, and incompatible tokenizers. A useful entropy-based comparison therefore needs
three adjustments: a common unit, fresh and relevant evaluation data, and protection against
memorized text.

Our framework supplies those adjustments. First, \textbf{Bits-Per-Byte (BPB)} normalizes
cross-entropy onto UTF-8 bytes, so each model is measured against the same physical amount of text
rather than its own tokenization. Second, a dynamic target-domain protocol replaces a permanently
fixed test with a recent corpus and records performance both before and after the same controlled
LoRA adaptation. Third, contamination filtering removes suspected pre-training overlap before any
sample can influence adaptation or scoring. The adapted BPB level is the primary selection score;
the zero-shot$\rightarrow$adapted reduction diagnoses how much corpus calibration the model needed.

Our contributions are: (1) an entropy-based evaluation protocol combining byte normalization,
dynamic target-domain evaluation, controlled adaptation, and contamination filtering; (2) an
11-model comparison spanning 0.5B--35B and three architecture families; (3) token- and context-level
analyses explaining calibration and transfer to 2{,}048-token inference; and (4) a three-corpus
equal-budget validation showing that adaptation reorders candidates while adapted performance tiers
transfer. Code, reproducible commands, and the public leaderboard are available at
\url{https://github.com/DavidAfonsoValente/entropy-bench}.

\section{Related Work}

\textbf{Entropy and task evaluation.} Language modeling was originally grounded in byte- and
character-level entropy \cite{shannon1951prediction}, while token-level perplexity depends on each
model's vocabulary and segmentation \cite{mielke2019perplexity, ahia2023languages}. Normalizing
log-likelihood by the UTF-8 byte restores comparability: The Pile popularized BPB as a
cross-tokenizer metric \cite{gao2020pile}, Paloma adopted it to measure LM fit
\cite{magnusson2023paloma}, and byte-level architectures remove the tokenizer entirely
\cite{xue2021byt5, pagnoni2024blt}. Community leaderboards measure complementary capabilities through
MMLU \cite{hendrycks2021mmlu}, MMLU-Pro \cite{wang2024mmlupro}, HellaSwag
\cite{zellers2019hellaswag}, and GSM8K \cite{cobbe2021gsm8k}, often using the
\emph{lm-evaluation-harness} \cite{gao2024lmeval}. We treat these as complementary diagnostics rather
than substitutes for the predictive objective. Our contribution is not BPB alone, but the protocol
that makes entropy actionable for model selection: byte normalization, fresh target-domain data,
controlled adaptation, a contamination gate, and cross-domain validation.

\textbf{Contamination and adaptation.} Large models verbatim-memorize pre-training text, increasingly so with scale and duplication \cite{carlini2021extracting, carlini2022quantifying}, making deduplication \cite{lee2022deduplicating} and contamination auditing \cite{xu2024survey} prerequisites for honest evaluation. Our audit combines the reference-free detectors Min-K\% and Min-K++ \cite{shi2023mink, zhang2024minkpp} with behavioral completion- and quiz-based probes \cite{golchin2023timetravel, golchin2025dcq}. Finally, domain-adaptive training reliably improves in-domain modeling \cite{gururangan2020dapt}, and scaling laws for transfer cast adaptation gains as a measure of a model's generality \cite{hernandez2021transfer}; we operationalize this cheaply and comparably across architectures via LoRA \cite{hu2021lora}.

\section{The Evaluation Pipeline}
\label{sec:pipeline}
The pipeline (Figure~\ref{fig:pipeline}) is a chronologically strict progression: no sample may influence adaptation or scoring before it clears the contamination gate.

\subsection{Phase 1: Forensic Contamination Audit}
We isolate data seen during pre-training with a hierarchical ``Scout-then-Verify'' framework:
\begin{itemize}[noitemsep,topsep=0pt]
    \item \textbf{Min-K++ Normalization (Scout):} We isolate tokens in the bottom $K=20\%$ of probability and normalize them against the unconditional vocabulary distribution ($\mu, \sigma$) at each step. High normalized likelihoods in historically rare tokens indicate verbatim memorization.
    \item \textbf{Contextual Confidence (CoDeC) (Scout):} We introduce CoDeC as an associative memory trigger. If a sequence $S$ was memorized, providing its immediate context $C$ causes a non-linear spike in predictive confidence ($\Delta_{\text{CoDeC}}$).
    \item \textbf{Dynamic Choice Quiz (DCQ) (Gatekeeper):} Suspected samples are presented alongside synthetic distractors generated by adjacent word-swapping and random \texttt{[PAD]} insertion. If the model deterministically selects the original string, it is quarantined. The reported runs use two distractors per sample (a $1/3$ chance level), the pipeline default.
\end{itemize}
The audit is run \emph{per model}, and the \emph{union} of all models' verified leaks is quarantined globally, so every model is adapted and scored on one identical, forensically cleansed corpus. Because memorized samples are removed up front, the zero-shot$\rightarrow$adapted BPB reduction reflects adaptation to the domain rather than recall of pre-training artifacts.

\subsection{Phase 2: Controlled Domain Adaptation}
The cleansed corpus is packed into contiguous $L{=}512$-token blocks along EOS boundaries to maximize gradient density. For the news study, model-specific Optuna sweeps with \textbf{Successive Halving} explore LoRA rank $r\in\{8,16,32,64\}$, $\alpha$-multiplier $\in\{1,2,4\}$, dropout, learning rate ($10^{-6}$--$10^{-3}$, log-scale), and effective batch size via gradient accumulation. Adapters are restricted to each model's language backbone, and the selected configuration is trained under a cosine schedule (decay to $0.1\times$ peak LR, gradient clip 1.0). The cross-domain confirmation in Section~\ref{sec:domains} uses one fixed configuration and the same 250-step budget for every model--corpus cell.

\subsection{Phase 3: Tokenizer-Agnostic BPB Evaluation}
Cross-entropy loss ($\loss_{\text{nats}}$) aggregated over the held-out corpus is unified onto byte-space using the UTF-8 byte-length ($|B|$) of the source text:
\begin{equation}
\bpb = \frac{\loss_{\text{nats}}}{\ln(2)} \times \frac{|Tokens|}{|B|}
\label{eq:bpb}
\end{equation}
Zero-shot and adapted BPB are both measured on the held-out test split, using a model-specific bytes-per-token factor estimated from the same test text, so scores are directly comparable within and across models. The packed test stream is evaluated as independent, non-overlapping 512-token blocks: losses are aggregated corpus-wide, while context resets at every block cut. Injected document-start markers remain available as context but are masked as prediction targets in the final cross-domain protocol. This makes the reported score an explicit 512-token-context comparison; Section~\ref{sec:tokengain} measures how performance changes within that window.

\section{Experimental Setup}
\paragraph{Models.} We evaluate 11 base models spanning dense Transformers, a sparse MoE, and a Liquid architecture (0.5B--35B parameters); the full cohort is listed in Table~\ref{tab:results}.

\paragraph{Data.} All models adapt to a single, semantically deduplicated slice of Google News dated 8 June 2026 (119{,}054 articles, $\sim$361\,MB of text), split $80/10/10$ into train/validation/test (seed 42) with an enforced zero-intersection constraint between splits.

\paragraph{Static benchmarks.} For the correlation study (Section~\ref{sec:corr}) we score every base model with the \emph{lm-evaluation-harness} (vLLM backend, bf16, greedy decoding, each tokenizer's native BOS) on MMLU-Pro (5-shot CoT, a fixed 1{,}000-question subset, exact-match), HellaSwag (10-shot, length-normalized accuracy), and GSM8K (5-shot, flexible-extract).

\paragraph{Hardware.} Adaptation ran under Slurm on the Leonardo (CINECA) GPU partition, 3 nodes $\times$ 4 NVIDIA A100 64\,GB; the static-benchmark suite ran separately on A100 GPUs.

\paragraph{Adaptation calibrates surface form.} Gemma-4-12B's largest movers are \texttt{<bos>}, punctuation, paragraph breaks, and common function words; LFM-2.5 shows the same pattern. Across the top 200 gaining tokens, formatting, punctuation, and function words account for $83\%$ of the gain for both models, while content words account for $5.2\%$ for Gemma and $14.0\%$ for LFM. Qwen-2.5-1.5B begins closer to the corpus distribution and allocates more of its smaller gain to dates and figures. The reduction therefore gives a useful measure of corpus calibration. For Gemma, the marker and its next 16 tokens contribute $3.6\%+5.0\%=8.6\%$ of the measured gain. This is a post-document-boundary window, not the first 17 positions of every block; the marker itself is excluded from the final cross-domain BPB.

\paragraph{Context reveals steady-state quality.} Figure~\ref{fig:blockpos} resolves loss and gain by position in the 512-token block and normalizes every bucket to UTF-8 bytes. Over the first eight positions, Gemma-4-12B averages $2.566$ bits/byte and Qwen-2.5-1.5B averages $1.490$; by positions $256$--$510$, Gemma reaches $0.717$ and Qwen $0.773$. The crossing therefore remains after byte normalization and is not explained by vocabulary size alone. Once context is available, the 12B model shows its stronger steady-state language modelling. The opening 32 positions account for $23.0\%$ of Gemma's total gain and $12.9\%$ of Qwen's, making context recovery a clear component of the calibration signal.

\section{A Reporting Standard for Entropy-Based Evaluation}
\label{sec:reporting}
The experiments support three conventions that make BPB comparisons easy to reproduce and explain.

\paragraph{Rank by adapted BPB.} The adapted level measures the model after the same kind of domain intervention used in deployment. The reduction from zero-shot BPB remains useful as a separate calibration diagnostic.

\paragraph{Handle document boundaries consistently.} Keep each model's document-start marker as context, exclude the injected marker from the scored target, and normalize BPB over the same scored positions. The cross-domain study applies this convention in all 33 model--corpus cells.

\paragraph{Keep denominators fixed.} Cross-domain ranks use all 11 models. Static-benchmark rank comparisons use the nine-model common subset reported across adapted BPB, MMLU-Pro, HellaSwag, and GSM8K, so the identical-rank counts (6, 2, 0) remain directly comparable.

\section{Domain Transfer: Stable Ordering Across Corpora}
\label{sec:domains}
Everything above rests on one domain. The claim under test is the one that would make BPB useful as a base-model selector rather than a news-specific instrument: \emph{newer and larger models from well-resourced labs should outperform older and smaller ones in adapted BPB, and that ordering should be stable across domains.} We prepared two further corpora to the same grouping and cleaning recipe --- exact deduplication on normalized text, Gopher quality heuristics, and MinHash/LSH near-duplicate filtering at a Jaccard threshold of $0.60$ --- then re-ran the cohort on all three.

moves from ninth on each zero-shot ranking to second, second, and fourth after adaptation. The

Every rank change occurs inside one of two near-tied clusters. The three mid-size models span $0.0259$ BPB on news and $0.0089$ on Hacker News; the two small models span $0.0258$ and $0.0088$. In both cases the spread narrows by roughly a factor of three. On Hacker News, Gemma-4-12B reaches $0.844$, only $0.009$ behind Ministral-3-14B and $0.008$ behind Qwen-3.5-35B-MoE. This leads to a practical rule: adapted BPB identifies the top model and stable performance tiers, while a target-domain run resolves choices inside a near-tied tier.

\paragraph{The architecture results are easy to place.} Below Gemma-4-31B, the adapted ranking follows model scale closely: the 12B, 14B, and 35B-MoE cluster, then 9B, 7B, 4B, the 1--1.5B group, and the 0.5B model. The sparse MoE scores alongside dense 12--14B models on every corpus, naturally locating its quality between its active and total parameter counts. LiquidAI-LFM2.5 shows a consistent $35$--$38\%$ calibration gain across domains, illustrating why the adapted level and the reduction provide complementary information.

\paragraph{Domain difficulty and model order separate cleanly.} All eleven models have their lowest BPB on news, highest on Reddit, and Hacker News between. Gemma-4-31B moves from $0.624$ on news to $0.913$ on Reddit, a $46\%$ increase, while the largest rank displacement is only two places. Absolute BPB therefore describes domain difficulty, and relative order identifies the model to choose.

\section{Conclusion}
Entropy-based evaluation provides a broad, direct signal of base-model quality, but it becomes a
fair model-selection tool only with the right protocol. Byte normalization makes cross-entropy
comparable across tokenizers; fresh target-domain corpora keep the measurement relevant;
contamination screening protects it from memorized text; and controlled adaptation measures the
model in the state in which it will be used.

The 11-model study gives a clear interpretation of every output. Adapted BPB is the primary ranking;
the reduction is a calibration diagnostic; the 512-token adaptation remains effective through
2{,}048-token inference contexts; HellaSwag confirms the language-modelling signal; and MMLU-Pro
and GSM8K add capability-specific views. Under one shared budget across news, Reddit, and Hacker
News, adaptation substantially changes the zero-shot candidate ordering, yet the resulting adapted
ranking remains highly stable and Gemma-4-31B ranks first on every corpus.

The practical workflow is simple: collect recent target-domain text, clean and audit it, adapt every candidate under one budget, rank by adapted BPB, and resolve near-ties on the target domain. This produces a repeatable base-model decision from data the evaluator controls.

\begin{abstract}
Fixed task benchmarks expose important capabilities, but their rankings conflict, their accuracy
scores discard most token-level evidence, and a single score cannot separate a stronger model from
one trained on more of the benchmark's subject matter: two models in our cohort sit $3.4\%$ apart in
adapted bits per byte and $2.0$ points apart on HellaSwag, yet $54.6$ points apart on GSM8K. Predictive cross-entropy uses every target token, yet
ordinary perplexity cannot compare models with different tokenizers. Bits per byte (BPB) fixes the
denominator; the open question is what to measure it on. We study that question directly, evaluating
11 base models (0.5B--35B parameters) on four fresh corpora---news, Reddit, Hacker News, and
arXiv mathematics---both zero-shot and after an identical LoRA adaptation, and comparing all 24
resulting rankings against MMLU-Pro, HellaSwag, and GSM8K.

The result is not that adaptation improves the ranking. It is that adaptation \emph{steers} it.
Zero-shot news BPB already selects the GSM8K and MMLU-Pro leader outright, with zero selection
regret. Adapting on general text moves the ranking toward conditional continuation---HellaSwag
agreement rises from $\rho=0.718$ to $0.982$---and away from the generative tasks, where agreement
falls to $\rho=0.727$ on GSM8K and $0.764$ on MMLU-Pro and the top pick now costs 2.3 and 4.2
accuracy points against the oracle. The same pattern holds on Reddit and Hacker News. Adapting
instead on contamination-screened mathematical prose---not on GSM8K questions---reverses it: GSM8K
agreement reaches $\rho=0.936$, the best pairwise accuracy in the study (50 of 55 model pairs), and
the top pick returns to the oracle, while HellaSwag agreement falls to $0.818$. Across four
adaptation corpora, the only one that beats doing nothing on GSM8K is the topically matched one.

Reported as selection accuracy over model pairs, with intervals from a cluster bootstrap over the
eleven models, the selection claim holds in $23$ of the $24$ (corpus, tier, benchmark) cells. The
contrast \emph{between} corpora does not separate from zero at this cohort size on any single pair
of them, which is why the steering claim rests on the four-corpus pattern rather than on one
comparison. Because the mechanism we propose is a family-level one, we also resample by publisher
rather than by model: the direction of the steering effect survives that stricter clustering---not
one of $20{,}000$ resamples reverses it---while its magnitude does not, and a leave-one-family-out
jackknife shows the magnitude concentrated in a single family.

We therefore describe base-model selection as a cost--fidelity frontier rather than a single score:
zero-shot BPB is free and a strong general-purpose selector, while adaptation costs compute and buys
alignment with whichever capability the adaptation corpus exercises. We position the result as a
second, unpreparable axis to read \emph{alongside} a benchmark scorecard rather than as a
replacement for it, and we bound its value honestly: over a cohort spanning $70\times$ in parameters
model size selects as well, and the metric earns its cost only among candidates of comparable size,
where size is a coin flip ($0.545$) and domain-matched adapted BPB reaches $0.818$. We report selection regret and
pairwise accuracy alongside rank correlation, and we release code, a versioned protocol, dataset
fingerprints, raw harness output, and every intermediate artifact.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}
For decades, language models were compared by predictive cross-entropy and its exponentiated form,
perplexity. The metric was broad and statistically efficient---every next token contributed
evidence---but token-level normalization made scores incomparable across vocabularies. Evaluation
then shifted toward fixed task suites. MMLU-Pro, HellaSwag, and GSM8K are useful precisely because
they isolate different capabilities \cite{wang2024mmlupro,zellers2019hellaswag,cobbe2021gsm8k}; the
same specialization also makes their rankings disagree. Once benchmark questions become development
targets or enter pre-training corpora, a high score may additionally reflect task preparation or
overlap rather than transferable base-model quality \cite{xu2024survey,zhang2025trainbeforetest}.

The difficulty is concrete rather than rhetorical, and it is visible inside this cohort. Llama-3.2-1B
and Qwen-2.5-1.5B sit at adjacent positions in our adapted news BPB ranking, $3.3\%$ apart, and
$2.0$ points apart on HellaSwag. On GSM8K they are $54.6$ points apart. Qwen-3.5-4B and
Llama-3.2-1B are $2.9\%$ apart on the same ranking and $73.9$ points apart on GSM8K. Two models can
therefore model fresh English almost identically well and differ nine-fold on a benchmark, and no
published score distinguishes ``this model is stronger'' from ``this model was trained on more of
this''. Both are legitimate design choices; the point is that the score cannot tell a reader which
one it is reporting, and the pre-training mixtures that would settle it are not disclosed. A metric
computed on text the evaluator owns, dated after every candidate shipped, cannot have been prepared
for---which is a different guarantee from being a better measure of quality, and we are careful
throughout to claim only the former.

Recent evidence makes the return to loss timely. Cross-entropy can have higher signal and lower noise
than accuracy \cite{heineman2025signal}, and train-before-test evaluations show that light,
task-specific preparation substantially harmonizes benchmark rankings and restores their connection
to perplexity \cite{zhang2025trainbeforetest}. The unresolved practical question is how to make that
connection useful when choosing among off-the-shelf base models with different tokenizers for a new
domain.

We answer with a controlled \emph{adapt-then-measure} protocol on evaluator-owned text. Bits per byte
(BPB) places every model on one physical denominator. A recent target-domain snapshot limits
staleness; internal deduplication and model-based risk signals trigger conservative quarantine before
training or scoring; and a fixed LoRA configuration measures candidates under the same data and
update budget. The endpoint, adapted BPB, answers ``which base model best models this domain after
equal preparation?'' The reduction from zero-shot to adapted BPB answers a different question:
``how much calibration did this corpus require?'' Neither number is presented as a substitute for a
task benchmark when the deployment task is already known.

The finding that organizes this paper is what happens when both endpoints are measured against
external benchmarks instead of only the adapted one. Adaptation does not move the ranking uniformly
closer to any external notion of model quality; it rotates the ranking toward whatever the
adaptation corpus exercises. That makes the choice of corpus the substantive decision in the
protocol, and it makes the zero-shot ranking---which is free---a serious baseline rather than an
intermediate quantity. We consequently report both tiers everywhere, and we judge each of them by
what it costs a practitioner who acts on it.

Our contributions are: (1) a tokenizer-agnostic, fresh-data protocol for comparing adaptable base
models, with a versioned estimand and auditable artifacts; (2) the complete
$4\times2\times3$ alignment matrix---four adaptation corpora, zero-shot and adapted, against three
benchmarks---which makes the zero-shot baseline and the non-matched-corpus control explicit rather
than assumed; (3) selection regret and pairwise accuracy as reporting statistics that a rank
correlation over 11 models cannot support on its own; (4) evidence that the adaptation corpus steers
which capability the ranking tracks, demonstrated by a math-domain intervention that is checked
against three general-text controls rather than one; (5) token- and context-level diagnostics of what
the adapters learn, including the finding that a fixed update budget measures calibration
\emph{speed} rather than attainable fit; and (6) a public implementation with benchmark manifests,
dataset fingerprints, raw harness output, and a validated leaderboard. Code and artifacts are
available at \url{https://github.com/DavidAfonsoValente/entropy-bench}.

\section{Related Work}

\textbf{From perplexity to reliable loss evaluation.} Language modeling was originally grounded in
byte- and character-level entropy \cite{shannon1951prediction}, while token-level perplexity depends
on each model's vocabulary and segmentation \cite{mielke2019perplexity,ahia2023languages}.
Normalizing log-likelihood by UTF-8 bytes restores a common unit: The Pile used BPB for
cross-tokenizer comparison \cite{gao2020pile}, and Paloma standardized broad, domain-resolved
measurement of language-model fit \cite{magnusson2023paloma}. Heineman et al. evaluate 465 models
over 30 benchmarks and show that loss-based metrics can offer better signal, noise, and scaling-law
reliability than accuracy \cite{heineman2025signal}. Our contribution is therefore not BPB itself;
it is the operational protocol around the metric for comparing heterogeneous, off-the-shelf base
models on new target data.

\textbf{Why task rankings disagree.} MMLU-Pro, HellaSwag, and GSM8K measure knowledge, conditional
continuation, and mathematical problem solving under distinct elicitation and scoring rules
\cite{wang2024mmlupro,zellers2019hellaswag,cobbe2021gsm8k}. Such scores can be indispensable for a
known deployment task, but accuracy is discrete and benchmark-specific. Observational scaling work
finds that a low-dimensional capability structure explains much public-model performance
\cite{ruan2024observational}, while controlled model families exhibit a smooth relationship between
validation loss and average downstream error \cite{gadre2025scales}. We test that relationship across
families and report both alignment and systematic residuals rather than collapsing the tasks into an
uninterpreted macro-average.

\textbf{Which corpus the loss is measured on.} That the agreement between predictive loss and
benchmark performance depends on \emph{where} the loss is measured is not new. Thrush et al. show
that per-domain perplexity correlates with benchmark performance to systematically different degrees
across web domains, and turn that dependence into a pretraining-data selection rule
\cite{thrush2024perplexity}. Their estimand is the complement of ours: they hold the benchmark suite
fixed and choose data to \emph{train} one strong model, using a pool of existing models to estimate
which domains carry signal. We hold the candidate pool fixed, adapt every member on the same corpus,
and ask how the resulting \emph{selection ranking} moves against several benchmarks at once. The
domain-dependence they document is the premise our steering result builds on; what we add is that
the dependence can be actuated---the corpus is a control input for which capability the ranking
tracks, with the matched corpus improving one benchmark's agreement while degrading another's, and
three general-text controls failing to do either.

\textbf{Equal preparation.} Dynamic evaluation updates a language model on recent test
history to improve predictive likelihood \cite{krause2018dynamic,krause2019dynamictransformer};
domain-adaptive pre-training improves fit to a target distribution \cite{gururangan2020dapt}.
Closest to our model-selection motivation, Zhang et al.'s \emph{train-before-test} gives 61 models
identical supervised, benchmark-specific preparation across 24 tasks: average cross-benchmark
Kendall agreement rises from $0.52$ to $0.76$, and pre-adaptation BPB predicts post-adaptation task
potential for base models \cite{zhang2025trainbeforetest}. Their result motivates our design but the
estimand differs. They rank post-fine-tuning task accuracy on public labeled benchmarks; we rank
held-out language-model fit after unsupervised adaptation to a fresh target-domain corpus. We also
standardize the byte denominator, screen contamination risk, and test rank transfer across raw-text
domains.

\textbf{Contamination controls.} Models can memorize duplicated pre-training text
\cite{carlini2021extracting,carlini2022quantifying}; deduplication and contamination analysis are
therefore necessary \cite{lee2022deduplicating,xu2024survey}. Reference-free membership signals such
as Min-K\% and Min-K++ are useful screens \cite{shi2023mink,zhang2024minkpp}, but no black-box score
proves that a sample occurred in a proprietary training set. We consequently use soft signals only
for conservative quarantine and report them as risk indicators, not verified leaks.

\section{The Evaluation Pipeline}
\label{sec:pipeline}
The pipeline (Figure~\ref{fig:pipeline}) is chronological: corpus construction and screening are
completed before any sample can influence adaptation or scoring.

\subsection{Phase 1: Deduplication and Contamination-Risk Screening}
We first enforce exact split disjointness and remove exact and MinHash/LSH near-duplicates. For
models whose pre-training corpora are unavailable, we then apply three reference-free screens:
\begin{itemize}[noitemsep,topsep=0pt]
    \item \textbf{Min-K++:} average the normalized likelihood of the lowest-probability $K=20\%$
    target tokens, using the model's probability-weighted vocabulary mean and variance at each
    position; unusually high within-split outliers become candidates.
    \item \textbf{Context sensitivity:} compare target NLL with and without randomly sampled
    same-corpus prefix documents; unusually large confidence gains add a soft risk signal.
    \item \textbf{Perturbation preference:} for already flagged candidates, compare the original
    sequence with two word-swap/placeholder perturbations. Preference for the original triggers
    conservative quarantine, but is not treated as proof of memorization because fluency alone can
    produce the same outcome.
\end{itemize}
The screen runs per model and the union of candidate indices is quarantined globally. Thus every
model receives the same retained corpus. This policy reduces overlap risk without making an
unverifiable claim about membership in proprietary pre-training data.

This screen is applied to the news corpus. The three later corpora are protected by a stronger and
simpler property instead: every document in them was published after every model in the cohort was
released, so no detector is needed to establish that they were not trained on
(Table~\ref{tab:corpora}). We regard freshness, not the detector stack, as the load-bearing
contamination defence in this work, and we recommend it over a screen wherever a target domain
permits a recent snapshot.

\subsection{Phase 2: Controlled Domain Adaptation}
The retained corpus is packed into contiguous $L{=}512$-token blocks along document boundaries.
Every headline model--corpus cell uses LoRA rank 16, $\alpha=32$, dropout $0.05$, learning rate
$10^{-4}$, effective batch 32, \texttt{all-linear} targets, and 250 update steps. This fixes the data
and update budget, although larger models still require more FLOPs and have more trainable LoRA
parameters.

\paragraph{Why a fixed configuration rather than a per-model search.} An earlier version of this
experiment selected each model's adapter by a multi-fidelity Optuna sweep. We abandoned that design
because its search budget was confounded with model size: within a fixed wall-clock allocation the
two largest models completed four trials each, while the 1B models completed 17 and 18
(\texttt{results/legacy\_sweep/*/trials.csv}). A search that is systematically deeper for small
models hands them a better-tuned adapter and biases adapted BPB by parameter count, by an amount
that cannot be estimated after the fact. One shared configuration removes that confound outright;
it is tuned to no model's advantage, and it is the reason the leaderboard is comparable at all. The
cost is that a fixed configuration is not equally well suited to every architecture, which
Section~\ref{sec:reporting} states as a limitation and Section~\ref{sec:convergence} proposes to
resolve.

Separately, the mechanistic diagnostics in Section~\ref{sec:tokengain} use three adapters
selected by that exploratory sweep; those runs explain the source of adaptation gains
but do not determine the public ranking.

\subsection{Phase 3: Tokenizer-Agnostic BPB Evaluation}
Cross-entropy loss ($\loss_{\text{nats}}$) aggregated over the held-out corpus is unified onto byte-space using the UTF-8 byte-length ($|B|$) of the source text:
\begin{equation}
\bpb = \frac{\loss_{\text{nats}}}{\ln(2)} \times \frac{|Tokens|}{|B|}
\label{eq:bpb}
\end{equation}
Zero-shot and adapted BPB are both measured on the held-out test split. Loss is accumulated over
scored tokens and divided by the exact UTF-8 byte count represented by those targets. The packed test
stream uses independent, non-overlapping 512-token blocks, so context resets at every cut. Injected
document-start markers remain available as context but are always masked as prediction targets in
the headline protocol. This point matters: an older exploratory run scored those markers; all core
tables, correlations, the manifest, and the leaderboard use the corrected marker-masked rerun.

\section{Experimental Setup}
\paragraph{Models.} We evaluate 11 base checkpoints spanning dense Transformers, one sparse MoE,
and one Liquid architecture (0.5B--35B parameters); the cohort is listed in
Tables~\ref{tab:domain_transfer} and~\ref{tab:bpb_static}.

\paragraph{Data.} The primary snapshot is Google News dated 8 June 2026 (119{,}054 articles,
$\sim$361\,MB of text). Reddit and Hacker News provide the transfer check in
Table~\ref{tab:corpora}. Every corpus uses an 80/10/10 train/validation/test split (seed 42) with
zero ID and normalized-text intersection across splits.

\paragraph{Static benchmarks.} For Section~\ref{sec:corr}, every base model is scored with
\emph{lm-evaluation-harness} 0.4.12 and vLLM 0.25.1 (bf16, greedy generation, tokenizer-native BOS).
MMLU-Pro uses 5-shot chain-of-thought and exact match on one stratified 1{,}000-question subset
(seed 42); HellaSwag uses 10-shot length-normalized multiple-choice accuracy on all 10{,}042
validation examples; GSM8K uses 5-shot chain-of-thought and flexible numeric extraction on all
1{,}319 test examples. These are directly observed scores under one harness, not vendor-reported
model-card values: the aggregate \texttt{results\_*.json} and per-example \texttt{samples\_*.jsonl}
for all 11 models on all three benchmarks are published under \texttt{results/gsm8k/},
\texttt{results/hellaswag/}, and \texttt{results/mmlu\_pro\_1k/}, so every number in
Table~\ref{tab:bpb_static} recomputes directly from the raw harness output
(\texttt{tools/verify\_paper\_numbers.py}).

\paragraph{Descriptive uncertainty and ranks.} We orient every rank so 1 is best, compute Pearson
correlation on score values, Spearman correlation on ranks, and Kendall agreement on pairwise order.
Wilson 95\% intervals show test-set sampling uncertainty for accuracy. They do not include
run-to-run, prompt, subset-selection, or model-family uncertainty; the cohort is purposive, so all
correlations are descriptive rather than population estimates.

\paragraph{Hardware.} Adaptation ran under Slurm on the Leonardo (CINECA) GPU partition, 3 nodes $\times$ 4 NVIDIA A100 64\,GB; the static-benchmark suite ran separately on A100 GPUs.

\section{Results and Discussion}

\subsection{Core Result: Adapted Rankings Transfer Across Domains}
\label{sec:domains}

does the ordering identify broadly strong base models rather than one news-specific winner? We
prepare three corpora with the same grouping and cleaning recipe---exact deduplication, Gopher
quality heuristics, and MinHash/LSH near-duplicate filtering at Jaccard $0.60$---and run all 11
models on every corpus. Table~\ref{tab:corpora} also records how each corpus is protected against
contamination, which is not uniform across the four and should not be read from
Figure~\ref{fig:pipeline} as if it were.

\paragraph{Adaptation changes the choice.} Zero-shot and adapted rankings are only moderately
aligned: Spearman $\rho=0.655$ on news, $0.609$ on Reddit, and $0.682$ on Hacker News, with maximum
movements of seven, seven, and five places. Gemma-4-31B moves from fourth, fifth, and sixth
zero-shot to first adapted; Gemma-4-12B moves from ninth in every zero-shot ranking to second,
second, and fourth. Adapted BPB is therefore not a rescaling of ordinary held-out loss; it estimates
fit after equal target-domain preparation.

\paragraph{The adapted order transfers.} News and Reddit agree on nine of eleven exact ranks
(Spearman $\rho=0.991$; maximum displacement one). News versus Hacker News yields $\rho=0.955$ and
Reddit versus Hacker News $\rho=0.964$, with maximum displacement two. Gemma-4-31B ranks first on
all three corpora. Figure~\ref{fig:crosscorpus} shows that the result is not only ordinal: Pearson
score correlations are $0.992$, $0.977$, and $0.990$.

\paragraph{Rank changes are near-tie changes.} The three-model mid-size tier spans $0.0259$ BPB on
news but only $0.0089$ on Hacker News; the two-model small tier spans $0.0258$ and $0.0088$.
On Hacker News, Ministral-3-14B ($0.835$), Qwen-3.5-35B-MoE ($0.836$), and Gemma-4-12B
($0.844$) are separated by $0.009$ BPB. A target-domain run should resolve choices inside such a
tier; the cross-domain result supports the broader tier ordering, not an immutable total order.

\paragraph{Difficulty and order are distinct.} Every model has its lowest BPB on news and highest
on Reddit. Gemma-4-31B moves from $0.624$ on news to $0.913$ on Reddit, a $46\%$ increase, yet
remains first. Absolute BPB describes domain difficulty and attainable fit; relative rank describes
which candidate is strongest under the shared adaptation protocol.

\paragraph{Adaptation does not improve the ranking; it rotates it.} Table~\ref{tab:alignment_matrix}
contains the result that organizes the rest of this paper, and it is only visible once the zero-shot
tier is reported alongside the adapted one. On news, adaptation raises HellaSwag agreement from
$\rho=0.718$ to $0.982$ and simultaneously \emph{lowers} GSM8K agreement from $0.836$ to $0.727$ and
MMLU-Pro from $0.855$ to $0.764$. The same three-way movement appears on Reddit
($0.655\rightarrow0.964$, $0.791\rightarrow0.691$, $0.791\rightarrow0.736$) and on Hacker News
($0.627\rightarrow0.973$, $0.827\rightarrow0.791$, $0.818\rightarrow0.845$). Three independent
corpora, one direction.

Table~\ref{tab:regret} states the same fact as a decision. Zero-shot news BPB selects
Qwen-3.5-35B-MoE, which is the GSM8K and MMLU-Pro leader---zero regret on both---at the cost of
$2.4$ points against Gemma-4-31B on HellaSwag. After adaptation the top pick becomes Gemma-4-31B:
zero regret on HellaSwag, $2.3$ and $4.2$ points of regret on GSM8K and MMLU-Pro. Adaptation did not
find a better model. It changed which question the ranking answers.

\paragraph{HellaSwag and BPB measure the same local object.} The direction of that rotation is not
mysterious. HellaSwag selects a continuation by length-normalized conditional log-likelihood, whereas
BPB aggregates conditional log-likelihood over every target token; further language-model training on
in-domain text should, and does, pull the two into near-identical order ($\rho=0.982$: seven models
keep their exact rank and none moves more than one place; Gemma-4-31B leads both at adapted BPB
$0.624$ and HellaSwag $87.9\%$). This is convergent validity between two measurements of the same
local object, and it is the least surprising cell in Table~\ref{tab:alignment_matrix} rather than the
protocol's strongest evidence. The generative benchmarks, which require producing and extracting an
answer, move the other way.

\paragraph{Knowledge and elicitation.} MMLU-Pro agreement remains substantial but
weaker ($\rho=0.764$). The Qwen-3.5-35B-MoE leads at $62.0\%$ and moves from BPB rank 3 to MMLU rank
1; Qwen-3.5-9B moves from 5 to 3. Gemma-4-12B moves from BPB rank 2 to MMLU rank 7. Unlike
HellaSwag's likelihood comparison, our MMLU-Pro protocol generates 5-shot chains of thought and
requires an extractable exact answer. The residual therefore mixes factual coverage, reasoning,
few-shot pattern continuation, and answer formatting.

\paragraph{GSM8K exposes a family-specific residual.} No model keeps the same rank. Every Qwen
model is unchanged or improves relative to adapted news BPB; both Gemma models decline, with
Gemma-4-12B moving from rank 2 to 7. The contrast is large even among small models: Qwen-2.5-1.5B
reaches $61.1\%$ GSM8K at BPB rank 9, while Llama-3.2-1B reaches $6.5\%$ at BPB rank 8. This supports
a narrow interpretation: arithmetic data, chain-of-thought preparation, and generation-format
compatibility vary systematically by model family. Architecture, data mixture, token budget, and
release generation are confounded here, so this correlational comparison alone cannot rank those
causes---which is what Section~\ref{sec:math} probes with an intervention on the adaptation corpus.

Two cautions belong with this residual. First, it is a residual against the \emph{adapted} ranking
specifically: zero-shot news BPB places Qwen-3.5-35B-MoE, Ministral-3-14B, and Qwen-3.5-9B in its top
three and orders 46 of 55 pairs correctly against GSM8K, so the family-specific disagreement is
substantially something adaptation introduces rather than something it reveals. Second, the
family claim rests on two Gemma models, which is an observation about this cohort and not yet a
demonstrated family effect.

\paragraph{The residual is a demonstration, not an estimate.} It is tempting to promote that
observation into a statistic: correlate $|$BPB gap$|$ against $|$benchmark gap$|$ over all $55$
pairs and report that BPB gaps predict HellaSwag gaps but not GSM8K gaps. The full-cohort
coefficients invite it ($r=+0.909$ for HellaSwag against $r=+0.266$ for GSM8K). We do not report
that contrast, because it does not survive deletion: dropping Llama-3.2-1B alone moves the GSM8K
coefficient to $+0.667$ and dropping Qwen-2.5-0.5B moves it to $+0.118$, a swing larger than the
coefficient's own distance from zero, while HellaSwag stays within $[0.859, 0.940]$ under every
single-model deletion. Each model sits in ten of the fifty-five pairs, so one unusual model moves
the fit a long way; this is the same leverage failure recorded for the BPB-movement correlation in
Section~\ref{sec:adaptedacc}, and it is reported here rather than avoided.

What survives is weaker in form and sufficient for the argument. Among the ten pairs within $5\%$
of each other on adapted news BPB, the six cross-family pairs have a median GSM8K gap of $20.0$
points and the four same-family pairs a median of $2.8$ points: when the metric says two models
from the same publisher are tied, the benchmark agrees, and when it says that about two models from
different publishers, it may not. Those are descriptive counts over single-digit cells. The named
pairs quoted in Section~\ref{sec:intro} are existence proofs about specific released models, so the
leverage argument does not apply to them, and they are all the motivation requires. All of it
regenerates via \texttt{tools/analyze\_selection\_divergence.py}.

\paragraph{Two benchmarks, not three.} MMLU-Pro and GSM8K are not independent evidence in this
cohort: they agree on 51 of 55 model pairs (Kendall $\tau=0.855$). Wherever both move together in
Tables~\ref{tab:alignment_matrix} and~\ref{tab:regret}---as they do in every row---that is one
finding reported twice, not two corroborating findings. The study therefore resolves two capability
axes, a generative one shared by MMLU-Pro and GSM8K and a likelihood one measured by HellaSwag.

\paragraph{Point-estimate leaders are not equally resolved.} HellaSwag's leader exceeds the runner-up
by $2.41$ percentage points (independent-binomial $z=5.03$), and their Wilson intervals do not
overlap. MMLU-Pro's $4.2$-point top gap has $z=1.92$ and overlapping intervals; GSM8K's top two Qwen
models differ by only $0.53$ points ($z=0.40$). Boldface therefore identifies the observed maximum,
not a universal or necessarily significant winner. The repo publishes the numerators, denominators,
intervals, ranks, and derivation script in \texttt{results/static\_benchmark\_analysis.json}, and the
raw harness output each of those numbers is computed from in \texttt{results/gsm8k/},
\texttt{results/hellaswag/}, and \texttt{results/mmlu\_pro\_1k/}.

\subsection{Math-Domain Adaptation Narrows the GSM8K Residual}
\label{sec:math}
Section~\ref{sec:corr} left the GSM8K residual unresolved: news-adapted BPB agrees with GSM8K rank
only moderately ($\rho=0.727$, 0 of 11 exact ranks), and we attributed this to a confound of
arithmetic data, chain-of-thought preparation, and answer-format compatibility that varies by model
family. That framing predicts the residual should \emph{not} respond to more adaptation unless the
adaptation corpus is itself math-domain text. We test this directly, outside the three-domain,
33-cell design of Section~\ref{sec:domains}: we repeat the adapt-then-measure protocol, with the same
fixed LoRA configuration as the other three domains, on a corpus of independent math-domain prose
rather than GSM8K questions. The corpus is 3{,}207 arXiv \texttt{math.*} titles and abstracts
submitted 9~June--20~August 2026---strictly after every model's release, the same freshness argument
used for Reddit and Hacker News---built with \texttt{tools/prepare\_math\_corpus.py} and screened
against the GSM8K test split by 10-word-shingle containment before any model saw it (0 hits), using
the same forbidden-corpus overlap check as the paper's contamination audit.

\paragraph{Judge it against doing nothing, not against news.} It is tempting to report this result
as $\rho=0.727\rightarrow0.936$, and an earlier draft of this paper did. That contrast overstates
the effect, because $0.727$ is not a neutral starting point---it is \emph{below} the $0.836$ that
zero-shot news BPB achieves with no adaptation at all. Roughly half of the apparent gain is
news-adaptation damage being undone rather than domain matching creating signal. Measured against
the do-nothing baseline the improvement is $+0.100$, and against every general-text corpus we tried
it is between $+0.145$ and $+0.245$.

A reader may reasonably ask why the do-nothing baseline for a \emph{math} corpus is zero-shot
\emph{news} BPB. It is because that is the harder test: zero-shot news BPB reaches $0.836$ on GSM8K
while zero-shot math BPB reaches only $0.800$, so scoring the intervention against the news baseline
subtracts more from it, not less. Using the corpus-matched zero-shot number would have reported
$+0.136$ rather than $+0.100$. We report the more conservative of the two.

\paragraph{The residual narrows, and HellaSwag pays for it.} What survives that correction is still
the strongest selection result in the study. Math-adapted BPB reaches $\rho=0.936$ on GSM8K with 6 of
11 exact ranks and 50 of 55 model pairs ordered correctly---the best pairwise accuracy of any variant
in Table~\ref{tab:alignment_matrix}---and it is the only adapted variant whose top pick is the GSM8K
oracle (Table~\ref{tab:regret}). MMLU-Pro moves with it ($0.764\rightarrow0.936$), which given the
$\tau=0.855$ redundancy between the two generative benchmarks is corroboration of the same axis
rather than a second result. HellaSwag moves the other way ($0.982\rightarrow0.818$), and we report
it with equal prominence: this is not a free improvement but a trade of general
conditional-continuation calibration for domain-specific calibration, the textbook cost of narrowing
what the adaptation data covers.

\paragraph{The reordering is mechanistically legible.} Under news-adapted BPB, Gemma-4-31B and
Gemma-4-12B rank first and second overall; under math-adapted BPB they fall to fourth and seventh,
while Qwen-3.5-35B-MoE and Qwen-3.5-9B rise from third and fifth to first and second. This is exactly
the correction GSM8K accuracy already called for: both Gemma models are the GSM8K-weak family
identified in Section~\ref{sec:corr}, and math-domain adaptation demotes them specifically rather than
reshuffling ranks arbitrarily.

\paragraph{Three general-text controls, and what they do and do not establish.} The obvious
alternative explanation is that any second corpus would have moved GSM8K. It would not:
Table~\ref{tab:math_rank_alignment} shows Reddit at $0.691$ and Hacker News at $0.791$, both at or
below the zero-shot baseline, against math's $0.936$. Two further checks address the remaining ways
the result could be an artifact of the cohort. Math-adapted BPB is not simply a cleaner copy of the
news-adapted ordering---the rank correlation between the two orderings is $\rho=0.755$, well below
the $\sim\!0.90$ that would indicate a sharpened rather than reordered ranking, with individual models
moving up to five ranks. And the improvement is not carried by one or two point movers: leave-one-out
recomputation, dropping each of the 11 models in turn, stays in $[0.915, 0.952]$, including when
either Gemma model is dropped (\texttt{tools/sanity\_check\_math\_domain.py}).

\paragraph{What the cohort size does not let us claim.} None of these checks makes the difference
statistically resolved, and we state that rather than let the leave-one-out result imply it. A paired
bootstrap over the model cohort (20{,}000 resamples, seed and code in
\texttt{tools/analyze\_alignment\_matrix.py}) puts the math-versus-news difference at
$\Delta\rho=+0.209$ with a 95\% interval of $[-0.096, +0.704]$, and the math-versus-zero-shot
difference at $+0.100$ with $[+0.000, +0.393]$. Both intervals include or touch zero, because a rank
correlation over 11 models cannot do better. The evidence here is the \emph{pattern}---four
adaptation corpora, one direction of effect, a reordering that demotes exactly the family GSM8K
already penalizes---and the pattern is what we ask the reader to weigh, not any single interval.
Resolving it properly requires a larger cohort, which Section~\ref{sec:reporting} names as the
first thing we would add.

\paragraph{Scope.} Two properties of the math corpus are worth stating precisely so the result is
reproducible rather than oversold. First, the corpus is smaller than the other three domains
($3{,}207$ documents, $15.1$\,MB, versus $119{,}054$ for news alone); the robustness checks above
were run at this scale and hold at it, though they test sensitivity to which model is included, not
to corpus size directly. Second, ``domain-matched'' here means topical match---arXiv research-paper
prose---not GSM8K's register of elementary word problems. That reflects a deliberate choice: the
corpus was built to avoid answer-format leakage (Section~\ref{sec:math}'s contamination screen), not
to mimic GSM8K's surface form. Whether a closer-format corpus would push the effect further is the
natural next experiment, not a gap in this one.

\subsection{Selection Accuracy, with Uncertainty at the Model Level}
\label{sec:pairwise}
Rank correlation over eleven models is a low-information summary, and pairwise selection
accuracy---given two candidates, does lower BPB pick the better one?---uses the comparisons a
practitioner actually makes. Each ranking induces $55$ pairs. It is tempting to test those pairs as
$55$ independent trials; that is invalid, because they are $55$ dependent comparisons among eleven
models, and treating them as independent lets one model's idiosyncrasy count as up to ten separate
pieces of evidence. We therefore resample \emph{models}: a cluster bootstrap over the eleven models,
$20{,}000$ draws, percentile intervals, the same unit of analysis as the bootstrap reported earlier.

\paragraph{The selection claim holds.} Selection accuracy exceeds chance in $23$ of the $24$
(corpus, tier, benchmark) cells, the interval excluding $0.5$ in every case but one. The strongest
cells are news- and Hacker-News-adapted BPB against HellaSwag ($0.964$, CI $[0.875,1.000]$ and
$[0.840,1.000]$) and math-adapted BPB against GSM8K and MMLU-Pro ($0.909$, CI $[0.755,1.000]$ and
$[0.784,1.000]$). The single exception is math-corpus \emph{zero-shot} BPB against HellaSwag
($0.727$, CI $[0.478,0.980]$), the pairing with the least reason to work. This is the claim the
leaderboard makes, and it survives testing at the unit the design randomises over.

\paragraph{The contrasts do not separate at this cohort size, and we do not claim they do.} The
difference in selection accuracy between math-adapted and news-adapted BPB is $+0.127$ on GSM8K
(CI $[-0.080,+0.367]$), $+0.127$ on MMLU-Pro (CI $[-0.060,+0.347]$) and $-0.127$ on HellaSwag
(CI $[-0.362,+0.062]$): the point estimates fall in the directions the matched-corpus hypothesis
predicts, in opposite directions on the two benchmark families, and every interval contains zero.
Adapting versus not adapting behaves the same way, $+0.109$ on GSM8K (CI $[+0.000,+0.286]$) and
$+0.164$ on HellaSwag (CI $[+0.000,+0.380]$). Eleven models is not enough to separate two corpora on
this statistic any more than on rank correlation, and switching statistics does not change that.
What carries the steering claim is the pattern across \emph{four} corpora reported in
Section~\ref{sec:math}---three general-text corpora all at or below the do-nothing baseline on
GSM8K, and only the topically matched one above it---not a significance test on any single pair of
them.

\paragraph{Three robustness checks that need no additional compute.} First, the steering
hypothesis predicts a \emph{joint} movement---toward the matched benchmark and away from the
other---so the better-powered statistic is a difference in differences: (GSM8K minus HellaSwag
selection accuracy under math adaptation) minus the same quantity under a general-text corpus. It is
$+0.255$ against news, $+0.255$ against Reddit and $+0.218$ against Hacker News, and $+0.242$
pooled.

The unit that statistic is resampled over decides what it means, and the obvious choice is wrong
here. Resampling the eleven \emph{models} gives strictly positive in $93.6\%$ of $20{,}000$ draws,
exactly zero in $5.1\%$ and negative in $1.3\%$ (one-sided $p=0.064$ counting every tie against the
hypothesis, $p=0.038$ under the mid-$p$ convention, $p=0.013$ counting none). But
Section~\ref{sec:domains} argues that GSM8K rank movement is a \emph{family} property---every Qwen
model holds or improves, both Gemma models fall---and a bootstrap that treats family members as
exchangeable draws contradicts the mechanism it is being used to support. Eleven models drawn from
five publishers is an effective $n$ well below eleven. We therefore report the family-clustered
resampling as the primary reading:

\end{center}

Two things follow, and they point in opposite directions; we state both rather than the convenient
one. The \emph{sign} is stable under the stricter clustering---not one of $20{,}000$ publisher-level
resamples is negative. The \emph{magnitude} is less resolved than the model-level figure implied:
under family clustering the effect no longer clears $0.05$ on any tie convention ($p=0.087$ and
$p=0.095$), where the model-level statistic did. These are not in tension, and neither rescues the
other: a sign that never flips across $20{,}000$ draws is evidence about direction only, and we do
not describe the result as more robust overall on the strength of it. The mass at exactly zero is
discreteness at five or six clusters, not degenerate draws.

A leave-one-family-out jackknife shows where the magnitude lives. Dropping the two Gemma models
takes the pooled difference in differences from $+0.242$ to $+0.056$; dropping all six Qwen models
takes it to $+0.133$, and dropping any of the three singleton families leaves it between $+0.222$
and $+0.296$. The steering effect is therefore concentrated in the family the paper already names
as the one whose GSM8K rank falls under general-text adaptation, which is coherent with the
mechanism but means the effect rests on two models.

That invites a fair charge of a double standard: Section~\ref{sec:domains} declines to report a
gap-size correlation \emph{because} one model's deletion moves it, while this section reports a
statistic that one family's deletion moves by a comparable fraction. The asymmetry is deliberate and
narrower than it looks. The correlation was a post-hoc addition with no pre-specified role, and a
single coefficient is the only form it could take---so publishing it would have meant publishing a
point estimate we could not defend. The difference in differences is the pre-specified primary
statistic, and we report it with its full jackknife attached and make \emph{no} significance claim
for its magnitude. Neither is offered as a resolved number; the difference is that one is reported
with its fragility in the same breath and the other could not have been. We report the direction as well supported and
the magnitude as unresolved, and note that this is the same $n$ problem as everywhere else in the
study: the remedy is more \emph{families}, not more models.

Second, corpus effects could in principle be confounded with optimisation randomness, since each
cell is a single adaptation run. They are not, to the precision that matters here: re-running two
Ministral-3-14B cells weeks later on different hardware reproduced zero-shot BPB exactly
($0.7181$ and $0.6718$) and adapted BPB to within $0.0003$ ($0.6788$ against a published $0.6791$;
$0.6221$ exactly). Run-to-run variation is two orders of magnitude below the effects being compared.

Third, the benchmark rankings are treated above as ground truth, but they are themselves estimates.
Recomputing selection accuracy while discarding model pairs whose benchmark gap is smaller than
$k$ combined standard errors---taken from the committed per-benchmark logs---leaves the result
intact and slightly improves it: on GSM8K $0.909$ at $k=0$ becomes $0.933$ at $k=3$, and on
HellaSwag $0.836$ becomes $0.863$. The errors the metric makes are concentrated in pairs the
benchmark itself cannot separate, which is the failure mode one would want.

\paragraph{Against the obvious baseline: model size.} A selection metric has to beat ``pick the
bigger model.'' Over the full cohort the two tiers answer that differently, and the distinction
matters. Parameter count orders $0.855$ of pairs correctly on GSM8K and MMLU-Pro and $0.964$ on
HellaSwag. The free tier loses to it everywhere ($0.836$, $0.836$, $0.800$ for zero-shot news BPB).
The paid tier beats it on both generative benchmarks ($0.909$ against $0.855$) and loses on
HellaSwag ($0.836$ against $0.964$). So the honest headline is not that size wins, but that size is
a strong and free baseline that only domain-matched adaptation improves on, and only on the
benchmarks the adaptation corpus is matched to.

Even that flatters size for a structural reason: this cohort spans $0.5$B to $35$B, a $70\times$
range, so ordering by parameters is close to ordering by capability and the question is not one a
practitioner needs help with.

The decision that is actually hard is between candidates of comparable size, and there the picture
reverses. Restricted to pairs within $2\times$ of each other in parameters, size is barely better
than a coin flip---$0.545$ on GSM8K and MMLU-Pro---while math-adapted BPB orders $0.818$ of the same
pairs correctly; within $1.5\times$, $0.714$ against $0.857$. HellaSwag runs the other way in every
band ($0.818$ against $0.545$ within $2\times$), which is the rotation of Section~\ref{sec:math}
appearing again: a metric steered toward mathematical prose orders reasoning better and commonsense
worse. The same restriction prices the two tiers against each other, which the cost--fidelity frontier
otherwise leaves qualitative. Within $2\times$, on GSM8K, picking the bigger model is right $0.545$
of the time, zero-shot BPB $0.636$, and math-adapted BPB $0.818$: adaptation buys roughly $0.18$
over doing nothing exactly where the choice is hard, against $0.07$ over the whole cohort where size
already answers the question. The value of the paid tier is concentrated in the close calls, which
is also where a practitioner would be willing to pay for it.

\paragraph{Against the alternatives a practitioner actually weighs.} Model size is the obvious
baseline but not the only one. Table~\ref{tab:alternatives} sets out the options and what each
one costs and tells you. The honest summary is that our method is not the best answer to every
version of the question: when the deployment task is known and you can afford it,
fine-tuning each candidate and measuring the task is strictly better evidence, and we say so. The
protocol here is the right choice in the specific and common case where the deployment task is not
yet a labelled benchmark, the candidates are close in size, and the corpus you care about is text
you own.

\section{Reporting Standard and Limitations}
\label{sec:reporting}
The evidence supports a practical reporting contract and also fixes the boundary of the claim.

\paragraph{Version the full estimand.} A leaderboard row is comparable only when corpus revision,
split, context length, marker masking, risk screen, adaptation configuration, update budget, and BPB
aggregation match. Any change creates a new benchmark ID. The public leaderboard validates these
fields rather than accepting an isolated number.

\paragraph{Separate level, change, and uncertainty.} Adapted BPB ranks endpoint fit;
zero-shot$\rightarrow$adapted reduction describes calibration. Task accuracies retain their own
denominators and intervals. A large reduction, bold point estimate, or tiny numerical BPB gap should
not be promoted into a stronger claim than the underlying comparison supports.

\paragraph{Scope of model selection.} Adapted BPB measures base-model potential after the specified
unsupervised domain intervention. Zero-shot BPB better describes immediate language-model fit, and a
task benchmark remains the relevant criterion when the deployment task and elicitation protocol are
known. The framework complements rather than replaces capability and safety evaluation.

\paragraph{Report both tiers, and choose between them on cost.} Table~\ref{tab:alignment_matrix}
makes the practical recommendation two-tiered rather than singular. Zero-shot BPB requires one
forward pass over held-out domain text and, in this cohort, already selects the generative-benchmark
leader on three of four corpora. Adaptation costs a training run per candidate and does not
dominate it: it buys alignment with whatever the adaptation corpus exercises, at the price of
alignment elsewhere. A practitioner who will ship the base model as released should read the
zero-shot column; one who will fine-tune on domain text should read the adapted column for
\emph{that} domain; and one who cares about a specific downstream capability should adapt on text
matched to it, as Section~\ref{sec:math} demonstrates for mathematics.

\subsection{What a Fixed Update Budget Actually Measures}
\label{sec:convergence}
The fixed 250-step budget makes cells comparable, but Section~\ref{sec:tokengain} shows what is being
compared, and it is narrower than ``fit to the domain''. Across the two adapters studied there,
roughly $83\%$ of the gain falls on formatting, punctuation, and function words, with content words
at $5.2\%$ and $14.0\%$; the opening 32 positions of a block account for $23.0\%$ of Gemma's total
gain. At 250 steps every model has consumed 8{,}000 blocks, which is $0.21$ epochs of the news
corpus. What that budget resolves best is therefore how quickly a model absorbs a corpus's surface
conventions---and the models that move most are exactly those that begin furthest from them
(Gemma-4-12B $24.5\%$ reduction, LFM2.5 $36.4\%$, every Qwen between $3.6\%$ and $5.4\%$).

This is a genuine limitation of the present estimand rather than a defect in the measurement, and it
has a clean resolution we did not have the compute budget to run here: adapt every cell to its own
convergence instead of to a shared step count. Concretely, replace ``250 steps at one configuration''
with ``the minimum test BPB over a small \emph{published} configuration grid, at the checkpoint that
minimises validation BPB, with early stopping.'' A fixed grid rather than an adaptive search matters
for the same reason the fixed configuration replaced the sweep: an equal count of adaptive trials is
not equal treatment, because the search path differs per model. Under that estimand the cheap
surface-form gain is exhausted for every candidate, unequal epoch counts across corpora become a
property of the corpora rather than a confound, and steps-to-convergence becomes a second reportable
axis---how expensive each base model is to bring to a domain---that the present protocol discards.
We specify it here so the current leaderboard is read as what it is: fit after an equal and
explicitly stated budget, not attainable fit.

\subsection{Direct Measurement of Adapted Task Accuracy}
\label{sec:adaptedacc}
Section~\ref{sec:math} argues from rank movement that domain-matched adaptation trades general
conditional-continuation calibration for task-relevant calibration. That is an inference about a
mechanism, and the runs behind the published leaderboard did not retain adapters, so it had never
been measured. We therefore repeated the news and mathematics adaptations retaining each adapter,
merged them, and scored the adapted models on GSM8K and HellaSwag under the published task,
few-shot, and extraction settings.

Two design points make the resulting deltas interpretable. First, the released weights were
\emph{re-scored on the same host and engine build} that produced the adapted numbers. This is not
bookkeeping: the identical released weights scored on our earlier hardware and on this one differ by
$-0.60$ to $+0.69$ accuracy points on GSM8K, which is larger than most of the effects being
measured, while HellaSwag---a loglikelihood task---moves by at most $0.05$. Any adapted-versus-base
delta that spans a hardware or engine change is uninterpretable at this scale, and cross-host
comparison is common practice. Second, cells whose base score could not be re-measured on the same
host are excluded from every aggregate rather than mixed in.

\paragraph{The ranking survives adaptation in the five models we could test.} The claim in this
subsection is scoped to that subset and should not be read as a property of the eleven-model
leaderboard: the six excluded models are excluded by loader faults that correlate with architecture
(a sparse MoE and multimodal-wrapped checkpoints), which is precisely the direction in which an
adaptation-induced reordering would be most likely to appear. The unit here is a
ranking, not a cell: four rankings of five models each---two corpora by two benchmarks---and
\emph{all four came back in exactly the base order}, Spearman $\rho=1.00$ with not one inverted
pair. A single such ranking arises by chance with probability $1/5!=0.0083$, so one ranking already
rejects at $p<0.01$; the four are dependent, sharing both models and benchmarks, so we read them as
one corroborated observation rather than four. It is a different measurement of the rank claim
rather than an independent replication of it---the same cohort, the same adaptations---and it is
evidence that these adaptation runs leave the ordering intact.

\paragraph{Absolute accuracy moves little, and by less than the cohort can localise.} Mean changes
are small and negative in all four cells, the largest being $-2.82$ points on GSM8K after
mathematics adaptation. Two observations bound how much weight that can carry. The paired
per-model contrast between the two corpora is $-1.47$ accuracy points with $t(4)=-1.63$, and only
three of five models fall on the negative side, so the cohort cannot separate the corpora on
accuracy the way it separates them on rank. And the natural mechanism---that degradation tracks how
far a model moved in BPB---is carried entirely by one model: Pearson $r=-0.935$ over all cells and
$-0.036$ once LFM2.5-1.2B is removed, that model reducing BPB by $36$--$42\%$ where every other
reduces it by $3.6$--$7.9\%$. A third check rules out the obvious artefact: answer-extraction drift
is real, in that the lenient-minus-strict extraction gap widens under mathematics adaptation, but it
cannot explain the change because the reported metric is already the lenient extractor.

\paragraph{Scope of this section.} We pre-registered that no probe-versus-stress-test verdict would
be issued until the full cohort was measured, and it was not: three Qwen3.5 and two Gemma-4
checkpoints are multimodal wrappers this inference stack cannot load, the latter because it refuses
a configuration whose per-layer attention width genuinely varies
($\mathtt{head\_dim}=256$ against $\mathtt{global\_head\_dim}=512$ over $40$ sliding and $8$ full
attention layers), and Ministral-3-14B's merged checkpoint requires a preprocessor configuration its
publisher does not ship. All six exclusions were diagnosed before any excluded result existed, so
their cause is independent of the outcomes, and each was refused a workaround that would have
produced plausible numbers from a silently wrong model. We therefore report the accuracy comparison
as directional and leave the mechanism question to the cohort extension of
Section~\ref{sec:convergence}, which is the experiment that settles it: on the observed effect and spread---themselves
imprecise at five models---separating the two corpora on accuracy would need a cohort on the order
of fifteen, which is an indication of scale rather than a target. Section~\ref{sec:math}'s
mechanism sentence stands as an inference, now explicitly labelled as one. The rank result above is
unaffected---it is complete on the models measured.

\paragraph{The validation is circular, and here is how far that goes.} The sharpest objection to
this study is that it validates a metric said to be resistant to benchmark preparation by measuring
its agreement with the benchmarks whose preparability motivates it. If those benchmarks are
trustworthy enough to serve as ground truth, they are trustworthy enough to read directly; if they
are not, agreement with them is agreement with noise. The objection is real and it bounds the claim
rather than dissolving it. Our answer is that the two readings operate at different levels:
preparation perturbs an individual model's score on an individual suite, whereas what we validate
against is a \emph{cohort ordering} aggregated over eleven models and three benchmarks, which is a
far more robust object than any single cell. Recovering that ordering from text no vendor could
have seen establishes that the axis is real. What it cannot establish is that BPB is \emph{more
correct} than the benchmarks where the two disagree; on this evidence, disagreement is a flag to
investigate, not a verdict in our favour. Settling that requires an outcome criterion independent
of the static suites---in-domain cloze on held-out corpus splits is the design we have specified and
not yet run---and until then the honest statement is that adapted BPB is an independent,
unpreparable second axis that is as informative as size where the decision is hard, not a
replacement for task evaluation.

\paragraph{Limitations.} The protocol is designed to be honest about what it does not show, which is
what makes the rest of it trustworthy.

\emph{Cohort size is the binding constraint.} Eleven models is too few to resolve differences between
rank correlations, as the bootstrap intervals in Section~\ref{sec:math} show, and the family-level
reading of the GSM8K residual rests on two Gemma models. Expanding the cohort is the single change
that would most improve this work, and it is cheap, because the models that tighten the statistics
are the small ones. The cohort is also English-centric and confounds architecture, data mixture,
token budget, release generation, and parameter count.

\emph{The external yardstick is domain-general.} We validate a domain-specific selection metric
against MMLU-Pro, HellaSwag, and GSM8K, none of which is about news, Reddit, or Hacker News. When a
domain-adapted ranking disagrees with a benchmark, this design cannot separate ``the metric was
wrong'' from ``the benchmark was the wrong yardstick for this domain''. Closing that gap requires an
automatic, in-domain, non-likelihood task---masked-entity or numeric cloze built from the held-out
split is the natural candidate, since it is uncontaminated by construction and scores exactly the
content positions Section~\ref{sec:tokengain} shows adaptation does \emph{not} improve---and that
experiment is not in this paper.

\emph{Measurement caveats.} Fixed update and data budgets are not equal-FLOP budgets, and
Section~\ref{sec:convergence} states what the budget does and does not measure. Evaluation blocks are
512 \emph{tokens} rather than a fixed byte span, so models with different tokenizers score slightly
different amounts of text (5.92--6.24\,MB, a $5\%$ spread) with block boundaries in different places;
using the measured context sensitivity of Figure~\ref{fig:contextlength} to bound the resulting
bias, it reaches at most $0.08\times$ the smallest adjacent rank gap and overturns no rank, but
byte-aligned blocking would remove it. Adapters were not retained, so no adapted model is scored on
a task benchmark anywhere in this work: the claim that domain-matched adaptation trades one kind of
calibration for another is an inference from rank movement, not a direct measurement of accuracy
before and after. Accuracy intervals cover test-set sampling only. The three diagnostic adapters and
64-window context study are explanatory probes, not cohort-wide replications. Reference-free
contamination signals justify conservative removal but cannot verify proprietary training
membership. Finally, the news text is third-party material: the release publishes its exact
record-level fingerprint and verification tooling, but not the raw text without redistribution
permission. These constraints limit causal and universal claims while leaving every reported
comparison auditable.

\section{Conclusion}
The old choice between incomparable perplexities and narrow task accuracies is unnecessary. A recent
raw-text snapshot, a byte denominator, conservative risk screening, and an explicitly stated
adaptation budget turn predictive loss into an auditable base-model selection experiment.

What the experiment shows is not that adaptation makes the ranking better. Measured against external
benchmarks, adaptation on general text makes it better on one axis and worse on the other: HellaSwag
agreement rises from $\rho=0.718$ to $0.982$ on news while GSM8K falls from $0.836$ to $0.727$ and
MMLU-Pro from $0.855$ to $0.764$, with the same pattern on Reddit and Hacker News. In selection terms,
the free zero-shot ranking picks the GSM8K and MMLU-Pro leader outright and the adapted ranking gives
up $2.3$ and $4.2$ accuracy points to pick the HellaSwag leader instead. Adaptation rotates the
ranking toward whatever the adaptation corpus exercises.

That is a more useful result than a uniform improvement would have been, because it is
controllable. Adapting on contamination-screened mathematical prose---not on GSM8K questions---puts
GSM8K agreement at $\rho=0.936$ with 50 of 55 model pairs ordered correctly, the best selection
performance anywhere in this study, and restores the oracle pick; the same swap costs HellaSwag
agreement, down to $0.818$. Three general-text corpora, tried as controls, all leave GSM8K at or
below the do-nothing baseline. The adaptation corpus is the instrument, and choosing it is the
substantive decision the protocol asks of its user.

The recommendation is therefore a frontier rather than a number. Rank by zero-shot BPB on fresh
target text when the model will be used as released---it is one forward pass and it is a strong
general-purpose selector. Adapt and rank by adapted BPB when the model will be fine-tuned on domain
text, reading the column for \emph{that} domain. And when a specific downstream capability decides
the choice, adapt on text matched to that capability and expect to trade breadth for it. Relative to
train-before-test, the distinction is labels and estimand: that work harmonizes ranks after
supervised task adaptation, whereas we compare off-the-shelf bases after unlabeled target-text
preparation, across tokenizers.

We are explicit about what would change these conclusions, and about what turned out not to need
changing. Eleven models cannot resolve a difference between two rank correlations, and our bootstrap
intervals say so rather than implying otherwise---but that is a limit of the statistic, not of the
evidence. Section~\ref{sec:pairwise} reports the more informative
statistic---selection accuracy over model pairs---with uncertainty still taken at the model level,
and the selection claim holds in $23$ of $24$ cells while the between-corpus contrast remains
unresolved. Testing those pairs as independent trials would make the contrast significant; it would
also be wrong, and we say so rather than banking it. Three limits remain. The external yardsticks are domain-general while the metric is domain-specific, and a fixed
step budget measures how fast a model absorbs a corpus's surface form rather than how well it can
ultimately model the domain. Each has a concrete remedy---a wider cohort, an in-domain generative
task, adaptation run to convergence---and the cohort is the one that would settle the between-corpus
contrast. The released code, manifests, and per-cell artifacts are arranged so
that anyone, including us, can run all of them against exactly these numbers.

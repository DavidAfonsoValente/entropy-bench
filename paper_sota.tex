\author{David Afonso Valente\\\normalsize Bottlecap AI}

\date{}

\maketitle
\begin{abstract}
A language model is trained to predict text and should be judged by how well it predicts the text
it will be used on. Base models are instead chosen by public benchmarks, which vendors optimise for,
which can leak into training data, and which score a model before the adaptation every team applies.
We return to the model's own objective. Every candidate is adapted to fresh, decontaminated text from
the target domain---its hyperparameters searched per model, then trained to convergence; for compute,
the runs reported here use one hand-picked recipe at a fixed budget---and ranked by held-out loss in
bits per byte, with no labels. Across seventeen base models and four corpora, adaptation reorders the
models, lifting a few that look weak out of the box to the top. The adapted ordering converges on
HellaSwag, the likelihood-scored benchmark, turns toward GSM8K and MMLU-Pro when the text is
mathematics, and stays stable across seeds, hardware and domains. Ranked before any task exists,
fifteen models land within one rank of where their fine-tuned news systems finish (Spearman $0.99$);
HellaSwag follows at $0.97$, while GSM8K and MMLU-Pro manage $0.59$ and $0.71$. Code and artifacts are
released.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}
Everything a base model can do, it learned by predicting the next piece of text. The most direct way
to ask how good one is, for a given use, is therefore to measure how well it predicts the text of that
use. That is not how base models are chosen. A new one ships most weeks, and what arrives with it is a
scorecard---MMLU-Pro, HellaSwag, GSM8K---read as the answer to \emph{how good is this model, for what
we do?} This paper argues that language models should be evaluated as language models: by the loss
they were trained to minimise, on the text they will be used on, after they have been adapted to it.

The scorecard misleads for two reasons. The first is that benchmarks are public. Each was written by
other people, for other purposes, and released before the model existed; vendors optimise for the ones
they report, and their items and subject matter can reach pre-training data. A high score is therefore
consistent with a genuinely stronger model or with one that saw more of that exam, and because
pre-training mixtures are not disclosed, no reader can tell which. Inside our own cohort,
Llama-3.2-1B and Qwen2.5-1.5B are
$2.1$ points apart on HellaSwag and $3.4\%$ apart in adapted bits per byte, yet $55.3$ points apart
on GSM8K: two models that model fresh English almost identically well differ by nearly a factor of
ten on one exam. The second reason is timing. Teams rarely deploy a base model exactly as it ships;
they adapt it to their data first, so what matters is how good a model \emph{becomes}. A model that has simply never seen prose like the target domain's can look weak out of the box and
still overtake one that looked stronger. Gemma-4-12B is the case in point (Figure~\ref{fig:headline}, circled): of fifteen models it is ninth
on GSM8K, eighth on MMLU-Pro and thirteenth on raw predictive loss, second once adapted, and it builds
the best fine-tuned system of all.

There is no universal ground truth for which base model is ``better''; a task tells us only which
model is better at that task, and tasks disagree. Loss is the principled yardstick. Every base model
was trained to minimise cross-entropy on text, so held-out loss is each model's own objective measured
out of sample, and every other ability it has was learned through that objective. Cross-entropy is
also a proper scoring rule: in expectation over text drawn from the domain's distribution $p$, a model
$q$ scores $H(p,q)=H(p)+\mathrm{KL}(p\,\|\,q)$, and $H(p)$ is the same for every model, so the
difference between two models' expected losses is \emph{exactly} the difference in how far each is
from the distribution that produced the text; held-out loss estimates it
\cite{shannon1951prediction}. Taking that seriously asks three things of the measurement. The loss is
counted in \emph{bytes}, because every model counts in its own tokens and bytes are the one unit they
all share. It is computed on \emph{fresh, decontaminated} text, so that no candidate has trained on
it. And it follows \emph{equal adaptation}: every candidate is adapted to the domain by the same
procedure---its own hyperparameter search, then training to convergence---so each model is measured
as it will be used rather than as it ships. Train-before-test \cite{zhang2025trainbeforetest} also
prepares candidates identically before comparing them, but with labelled benchmark data; we do it
unsupervised, on fresh text, and in bytes.

We apply the protocol to seventeen base models from eight families (0.5B--35B) on four corpora, and
four things follow. \textbf{Adaptation reorders the models.} The unadapted and adapted rankings agree
only at Spearman $0.61$--$0.68$ on general text, because unadapted loss mixes familiarity with a style
of prose into capability; the only models that climb are the three adaptation helps most
(Section~\ref{sec:mechanism}). \textbf{The adapted ordering converges on the language benchmark, and
can be aimed.} It matches HellaSwag at Spearman $0.96$--$0.98$ on news, Reddit and Hacker News, up
from $0.63$--$0.72$, and adapted on mathematics it swings to GSM8K and MMLU-Pro ($0.91$ and $0.93$;
Figure~\ref{fig:steering}, Section~\ref{sec:agreement}). \textbf{It is stable where benchmarks are
not.} Seed-to-seed variation is a thousandth of the spread between models, cells reproduce across
hardware, and the ordering carries across domains, while identical weights move GSM8K by up to
$0.69$ points when only the host changes (Section~\ref{sec:stability}). \textbf{Minutes of adaptation
anticipate hours of fine-tuning.} A team will fine-tune its chosen model anyway; measured before any
task exists, adapted bits per byte ranks fifteen models at Spearman $0.99$ with where their fine-tuned systems
finish---every model within one rank, eleven exactly. HellaSwag, the likelihood benchmark, follows at
$0.97$; the reasoning benchmarks manage $0.59$ (GSM8K) and $0.71$ (MMLU-Pro)
(Figure~\ref{fig:headline}, Section~\ref{sec:divergence}). Section~\ref{sec:protocol} gives the protocol
and Section~\ref{sec:results} the results in this order. Every number recomputes from a committed
artifact: \url{https://github.com/DavidAfonsoValente/entropy-bench}.

\section{The Protocol}
\label{sec:protocol}
The measurement runs in three phases, and the order is load-bearing: the corpus is fixed and screened
before any model touches it.
\label{sec:phases}

\paragraph{Phase 1: a decontaminated corpus.} Evaluation text must be text no candidate has seen,
and we guarantee it three ways. \emph{Time:} the text is dated after every candidate's release---Reddit and Hacker News are scraped in August 2026, after every model in the
cohort shipped, and the arXiv titles and abstracts run from 9 June to 20 August. \emph{Hygiene:} exact duplicates, text failing the Gopher quality heuristics and MinHash/LSH
near-duplicates at Jaccard $0.60$ are removed, and the 80/10/10 split shares no identifier or
normalised text across splits, so nothing in the test split reappears in training. \emph{Membership screens:} the news snapshot
(Google News, 8 June 2026, $119{,}054$ articles) predates part of the release window, so every model
is also tested for having seen it. Three reference-free tests look for the signatures memorised text
leaves: Min-K++ \cite{zhang2024minkpp} flags passages whose least likely tokens are unusually likely
for the model; context sensitivity flags passages whose loss falls more than expected when the model
is shown other text from the same corpus; and a perturbation test checks whether the model prefers the
original passage over copies with swapped words. The \emph{union} of everything any model flags---$80$
sequences---is removed for every model, so every candidate is measured on one identical corpus
(Appendix~\ref{app:robust}).

\paragraph{Phase 2: equal adaptation.} Every candidate is adapted to the retained training text,
packed into contiguous $512$-token blocks, with LoRA \cite{hu2021lora} on all linear layers, and
every candidate is adapted by the same procedure. Its hyperparameters come from its own Optuna search
\cite{akiba2019optuna}: a TPE sampler proposes the learning rate, batch size, warm-up, weight decay,
LoRA rank, $\alpha$ and dropout; successive halving prunes weak trials from $50$ steps on; and every
trial is scored by BPB on the validation split, never the test split. Every model receives the same
number of trials and no time limit: a search capped by wall-clock time lets small models finish more
trials than large ones and so tunes them better, a size bias we observed directly
(Appendix~\ref{app:robust}). The chosen configuration is then trained until validation BPB stops
improving, so each candidate is measured near the best it can reach on the domain. The released pipeline implements this procedure (\texttt{--hparams sweep --no-time-limit}), and
also accepts a hand-picked configuration in place of the search.

Searching seventeen models of up to 35B parameters in full was beyond our compute budget, so the
results reported here use that second path: one hand-picked configuration for every candidate---LoRA
rank 16, $\alpha{=}32$, dropout $0.05$, learning rate $10^{-4}$, effective batch 32---and a fixed $250$
update steps rather than training to convergence. That is $4.1$M training tokens per candidate, which
on one A100 cost $3.7$ GPU-minutes at $0.5$B, $11.6$ at $7.7$B, $24.7$ at $12$B and $62.7$ at $31$B.
The configuration sits where our own earlier search settled---rank 16 won for eight of the eleven
models and the median winning learning rate was $1.1\times10^{-4}$---and its endpoint is stable to
seed and hardware (Section~\ref{sec:stability}), but it suits no architecture perfectly, and the full
procedure can only tune each candidate closer to its best.

\paragraph{Phase 3: bits per byte.} Cross-entropy on the held-out test split is divided by the
UTF-8 byte length $|B|$ of the text those predictions cover:
\begin{equation}
\bpb = \frac{\loss_{\text{nats}}}{\ln 2} \times \frac{|\text{Tokens}|}{|B|}
\label{eq:bpb}
\end{equation}
The byte count is a property of the text and identical for every candidate, so a larger vocabulary
earns no advantage the way it would under per-token perplexity. Test blocks are independent and
non-overlapping, so context resets at every cut, and injected document-start markers stay available
as context but are always masked as prediction targets. We report both endpoints, before adaptation
and after, because the difference between them is itself informative (Section~\ref{sec:mechanism}).

\paragraph{Cohort and setup.} We evaluate 11 base checkpoints spanning dense Transformers, one sparse
MoE and one Liquid architecture (0.5B--35B parameters; Table~\ref{tab:leaderboard}) on all four
corpora, and extend the news cohort to 17 on preregistered size-adjacency and publisher-spread
criteria (Table~\ref{tab:cohort}), from the Llama, Qwen, Gemma, Ministral, LiquidAI, Falcon, Mistral
and SmolLM families
\cite{dubey2024llama3,yang2024qwen25,gemma2026gemma4,liu2026ministral3,qwen2026qwen35}. For comparison
every model is also scored on MMLU-Pro \cite{wang2024mmlupro}, HellaSwag \cite{zellers2019hellaswag}
and GSM8K \cite{cobbe2021gsm8k} with \emph{lm-evaluation-harness} 0.4.12 \cite{gao2024lmeval} on one
host and one build, under the protocol of Appendix~\ref{app:rescore}---directly observed scores rather
than vendor-reported card values, with the harness logs published so each recomputes from raw output.
Adaptation ran on Leonardo (CINECA), 3 nodes $\times$ 4 A100 64\,GB. Intervals resample models, never
pairs, because pairs that share a model are not independent ($20{,}000$ draws, percentile, clustered);
intervals on one fine-tuned pair resample its $500$ evaluation articles.

\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

\subsection{Adaptation reorders the models}
\label{sec:mechanism}
Adapting every model before measuring does not just lower every score; it reorders the models. On
news the unadapted and adapted rankings of the eleven models agree only at Spearman $0.655$, on Reddit
$0.609$ and on Hacker News $0.682$, and a model can move seven places. The movement is not spread
evenly. Adaptation lowers loss by $3.6$--$5.4\%$ on news for every Qwen, Llama and Ministral model,
but by $17.0\%$ for Gemma-4-31B, $24.5\%$ for Gemma-4-12B and $36.4\%$ for LFM2.5-1.2B---and those
three are the only models that move up; every other model slips one or two places to make room. Gemma-4-12B is ninth of eleven unadapted and second
adapted, a $12$B model the unadapted reading puts below two models under $2$B; LFM2.5-1.2B, the one
non-Transformer, is dead last unadapted at $1.34$ bits per byte against a $0.5$B Qwen's $0.93$, and
ahead of it after adaptation.

The reason is familiarity. Zero-shot loss conflates two things that need separating: \emph{this model
already writes like my corpus} and \emph{this model is a capable model}. Figure~\ref{fig:blockpos}
shows the confound in the raw loss, resolved by position inside a packed evaluation block. Because
blocks are independent, every block is a cold start. Unadapted, Gemma-4-12B opens a block at $2.83$
bits per byte where Qwen-2.5-1.5B---eight times smaller---opens at $2.04$, and it stays behind for
roughly the first half of the block; by the end of the block it has passed it, $0.72$ against $0.77$.
A block-averaged number therefore reports mostly how quickly a model finds its footing in unfamiliar
prose---the document conventions, formatting and register of this corpus---which is a real property
but not the one a choice of model turns on. A short equal adaptation supplies exactly the missing
familiarity, and the reduction it buys is largest in the opening but does not vanish after it: what
is left is closer to capability. Unadapted loss is not wrong, then, but it answers a different
question---how much this model already resembles the target text---and the models it penalises most
are those whose pre-training prose differs most from it, which is also why a non-Transformer with its
own training mixture is hit hardest.

\subsection{The adapted ordering converges on the language benchmark, and can be aimed}
\label{sec:agreement}
Of the three public benchmarks, HellaSwag is the one closest to language modelling: it asks which
ending of a passage is most natural, and the harness scores it by the likelihood of that ending.
Before adaptation, BPB on news, Reddit and Hacker News ranks the eleven models at Spearman
$0.63$--$0.72$ with HellaSwag; after adaptation the same rank correlation is $0.96$--$0.98$, and on
news the adapted ranking orders $53$ of HellaSwag's $55$ pairs the same way (Table~\ref{tab:alignment};
benchmark scores in Table~\ref{tab:static}). Three independently collected corpora arrive at the same
ordering once familiarity is removed, and it is the ordering of the benchmark that measures the same
thing. Because HellaSwag and BPB are both likelihoods, this is consistency rather than a second
opinion: measuring on fresh text of one's own gives up nothing the likelihood benchmark captures,
while escaping everything that makes a public benchmark suspect.

The effect is specific to what the text is about. On general text the correlation with GSM8K falls on
all three corpora, and with MMLU-Pro on two of them; adapted on arXiv mathematics instead, BPB swings
toward the reasoning benchmarks---Spearman $0.91$ with GSM8K and $0.93$ with MMLU-Pro, up from $0.76$
and $0.74$---and ranking by mathematics BPB picks GSM8K's own top model at zero regret. The choice of
text points the measurement at a capability: the same instrument, fed news, recovers the language
benchmark, and fed mathematics, recovers the reasoning ones. Benchmarks cannot be aimed this way,
because each is fixed; a corpus is chosen by whoever is choosing the model.

\subsection{It is stable where benchmarks are not}
\label{sec:stability}
A ranking is only as useful as it is repeatable. Three preregistered seed replicates of all $17$
news cells put the median within-model standard deviation of adapted BPB at $0.000087$ against a
between-model spread of $0.074340$, a ratio of $0.0012$, and not one of the $40$ size-matched pairs (models within $2\times$ in parameters) reverses its order
across seeds; the one model where seed noise is visible at all is Gemma-4-12B,
by up to $1.24\%$. Re-running two cells weeks later on different hardware reproduced zero-shot BPB
exactly and adapted BPB to within $0.0003$, and a cell re-run through a different software stack
reproduced to $0.01\%$. The ordering also carries across domains: adapted rankings agree at
$\rho=0.991$ between news and Reddit, $0.955$ between news and Hacker News and $0.964$ between Reddit
and Hacker News (Figure~\ref{fig:crosscorpus}). The unadapted ordering is just as stable across
domains---$0.982$, $0.964$ and $0.973$---so adaptation does not buy stability; it buys a \emph{different}
stable ordering, the one Section~\ref{sec:mechanism} explains and Section~\ref{sec:agreement} shows
converging on the language benchmark.

Benchmark scores do not have this property. Identical released weights move $-0.60$ to $+0.69$ GSM8K
points when only the host changes, A100 to H100, and one decoding setting a checkpoint ships with was
worth $9.86$ GSM8K and $14.30$ MMLU-Pro points to Gemma-4-12B in our re-score
(Appendix~\ref{app:rescore}). A generative benchmark measures the model, the harness, the decoding
configuration and the hardware at once; bits per byte on a fixed corpus measures the model.

\subsection{Minutes of adaptation anticipate hours of fine-tuning}
\label{sec:divergence}
Whichever model a team chooses, it will then train that model for its task. The practical question is
whether the cheap measurement already says how that will go. We fine-tuned fifteen of the seventeen
models---every one that fits a single GPU---on a news task, writing the opening paragraph of an article
from its body, with one identical LoRA recipe and ROUGE-L against the human-written lede on $500$ held-out
articles from a split the BPB test blocks do not touch; the analysis was fixed in code before the jobs
ran (Appendix~\ref{sec:downstream}).
\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{figures/fig_downstream.pdf}
\caption{\textbf{Does each selector agree with the fine-tuned outcome?} Every size-matched pair of the
fifteen fine-tuned models, oriented so the selector prefers the first: $x$ is how strongly it
prefers it, $y$ how much better that model's fine-tuned system scored. Above the line is a pair the selector orders as the fine-tune does; filled markers are pairs the
fine-tune itself resolves. Adapted BPB agrees on $37$ of the $39$ pairs, and on $21$ of the $22$ the
fine-tune separates; HellaSwag follows at $34$. GSM8K agrees on $18$ of $38$, and every one of its
eleven gaps between $15$ and $25$ points sits \emph{below} the line, on a pair where it favoured the
model with the weaker system.}
\label{fig:downstream}
\end{figure}
Over all $39$ size-matched pairs the fifteen systems form, adapted BPB, measured with no task and no
labels, orders $0.949$ of the pairs as the fine-tune does, $[0.817, 1.000]$; HellaSwag orders $0.872$,
while GSM8K and MMLU-Pro sit at $0.474$ and $0.513$---a coin (Figure~\ref{fig:downstream},
Table~\ref{tab:downstream}). On the pairs the fine-tune itself separates it agrees in $21$ of the
$22$, and its ranking of the fifteen correlates with their final order at Spearman $0.99$, every model
within one rank and eleven exactly, where raw loss manages $0.71$, GSM8K $0.59$, MMLU-Pro $0.71$ and
HellaSwag $0.97$ (Figure~\ref{fig:headline}). The lead over ranking by size resolves under this recipe, $+0.231$
$[+0.059, +0.452]$. A deliberately different recipe---four times the rank, three times the learning
rate, half the batch, a new seed---leaves the systems' ordering almost unchanged (Spearman $0.982$) and
adapted BPB still first at $0.923$, in agreement on all $20$ pairs that recipe separates. That the two
agree is expected---both adapt the model to news---and it is the point: the few minutes of unlabelled
adaptation behind a BPB number already contain most of what hours of labelled fine-tuning reveal, and
the benchmarks read before choosing contain almost none of it. \textbf{Gemma-4-12B}, $9$th and $8$th of
fifteen on GSM8K and MMLU-Pro, builds the best system of the fifteen, beating Ministral-3-14B by
$+0.018$ $[+0.011, +0.025]$; and the $55.3$-point GSM8K gap between Llama-3.2-1B and Qwen-2.5-1.5B buys
no measurable difference once both are fine-tuned. Every model improved on its own base weights and
cleared what copying the article's first sentence scores ($0.125$).

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:using}
Language models should be evaluated as language models: by the loss they were trained to minimise, in
bits per byte, on fresh decontaminated text from the domain of use, after the same adaptation for every
candidate. There is no ground truth to appeal to, and the case rests on principle---the loss is every
model's own objective, and its differences are differences in distance from the text---and on what the
measurement reveals. Unadapted loss confuses familiarity with capability; adaptation removes the
familiarity and reorders the models. The adapted ordering converges on the likelihood benchmark on general text (Spearman
$0.96$--$0.98$) and on the reasoning benchmarks on mathematics, so it can be aimed at the capability that
matters. It is stable to seed, hardware and domain where benchmark scores move with the host. And it
anticipates how models fare once fine-tuned, where the reasoning benchmarks do not.

In practice the measurement is cheap enough to run on every candidate. When candidates differ by more
than about $2\times$ in parameters the larger one is usually the better choice, and the measurement
earns its cost where size has stopped deciding. Between two or three candidates the \emph{gap} is what
matters: a different seed moves adapted BPB by up to $1.24\%$ (Gemma-4-12B; the median model,
$0.01\%$) and token-aligned blocks by $0.18\%$, so
we treat a gap under $2\%$ as a tie, to be settled by licence, latency or serving cost. The unadapted number costs one forward pass and is best read as a screen
for a candidate badly off-distribution for the target text, not as the choice. Results are comparable only when corpus, split, context, masking and adaptation all match, so the
released benchmark keeps one leaderboard per corpus and recipe: a news track run by the maintainers,
and a mathematics track on arXiv titles and abstracts whose corpus is public, so that anyone can
evaluate a model and submit it.

Three objections deserve answers. \emph{Why not simply fine-tune on the task?} When the task, its
labels and the budget exist, that is the better evaluation for that task; a base model is usually
chosen before any of them do, and must serve many tasks at once. \emph{Does one hand-picked recipe
favour some architectures?} It suits none perfectly, which is why the method searches each model; the
reported ordering is stable to seed, and a second fine-tuning recipe leaves the downstream ordering at
Spearman $0.982$. \emph{Contamination} is addressed by dating, deduplication and membership screens.
What remains is breadth: seventeen models is enough to see these patterns and not yet enough to
resolve every gap, and a larger, more diverse cohort is the next step.

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}
\textbf{From perplexity to comparable loss.} Language modelling was grounded in byte- and
character-level entropy from the start \cite{shannon1951prediction}, but token-level perplexity
depends on each model's vocabulary \cite{mielke2019perplexity,ahia2023languages}; normalising by
UTF-8 bytes restores a common unit \cite{gao2020pile,magnusson2023paloma}, and loss-based metrics
carry better signal and lower noise than accuracy across 465 models \cite{heineman2025signal}. Our
contribution is the operational protocol around the metric, not the metric.

\textbf{Where the loss is measured matters.} Thrush et al.\ turn the varying correlation between
per-domain perplexity and benchmark performance into a pretraining-data selection rule
\cite{thrush2024perplexity}. Their estimand is the complement of ours: they fix the benchmark suite
and choose data to \emph{train} one strong model, where we fix the candidate pool and ask how the
\emph{selection ranking} moves.

\textbf{Equal preparation.} Dynamic evaluation updates a model on recent test history
\cite{krause2018dynamic,krause2019dynamictransformer} and domain-adaptive pretraining improves fit to
a target distribution \cite{gururangan2020dapt}. Closest to our motivation, \emph{train-before-test}
gives 61 models identical supervised preparation across 24 tasks and raises cross-benchmark Kendall
agreement from $0.52$ to $0.76$ \cite{zhang2025trainbeforetest}; they rank post-fine-tuning accuracy
on public labelled benchmarks, where we rank held-out fit after \emph{unsupervised} adaptation to a
fresh corpus, across tokenizers. The core idea---prepare every candidate identically before
comparing them---is theirs; what we add is doing it without labels, on text no vendor has seen, in a
unit that compares tokenizers, and checking the result against real fine-tuned systems. Controlled
families show a smooth loss-to-error relation
\cite{gadre2025scales,ruan2024observational}; we test it \emph{across} families.

\textbf{Contamination.} Models memorise duplicated pretraining text
\cite{carlini2021extracting,carlini2022quantifying}, so deduplication and contamination analysis are
necessary \cite{lee2022deduplicating,xu2024survey}; reference-free membership signals are useful
screens \cite{shi2023mink,zhang2024minkpp} but prove nothing about a proprietary training set, so we
use them only for conservative quarantine.

\section{Robustness and Measurement Artefacts}
\label{app:robust}

\paragraph{Stability across domains.}\label{sec:domains} All eleven models ran on three independently collected domains under the same recipe
(Tables~\ref{tab:corpora} and~\ref{tab:leaderboard}). Adaptation genuinely changes the
choice---zero-shot and adapted rankings agree only at Spearman $\rho = 0.655$ on news, $0.609$ on
Reddit and $0.682$ on Hacker News, with models moving up to seven places---and the adapted ordering
then holds across domains: news and Reddit agree on nine of eleven exact ranks
($\rho = 0.991$, maximum displacement one), news against Hacker News gives $0.955$ and Reddit
against Hacker News $0.964$, and the agreement is not merely ordinal (Figure~\ref{fig:crosscorpus}).
\textbf{So does the zero-shot ordering}---$0.982$, $0.964$ and $0.973$ on the same three
comparisons. The tempting story is that adaptation buys stability, and
it does not; it buys a \emph{different} stable ordering, and Section~\ref{sec:mechanism} is the
evidence that it is the better of the two.

\paragraph{Why the search gives every model the same number of trials.} The search of
Phase~2 uses a multivariate TPE sampler (seed $42$) over learning rate ($10^{-6}$--$10^{-3}$, log),
physical batch ($2$, $4$, $8$), gradient accumulation ($1$--$16$), warm-up ratio ($0$--$0.2$), weight
decay ($10^{-5}$--$10^{-1}$, log), LoRA rank ($8$--$64$), $\alpha$ as a multiple of rank ($1$, $2$,
$4$) and dropout ($0$--$0.2$); a successive-halving pruner starts trials at $50$ steps and promotes the
best third at each rung; and the objective is BPB on a fixed $256$-block subset of the validation
split. An earlier run of this search on the eleven-model news cohort was capped by wall-clock time
rather than by trial count, and the cap was confounded with model size: the two largest models
completed four trials each while the 1B models completed 17 and 18
(\texttt{results/legacy\_sweep/*/trials.csv}), and the five models of 9B or more all kept the same
third draw of the seeded sampler as their winner, while the smaller models' winners came from much
later in their searches. A time-capped search hands small models a better-tuned adapter and biases
the ranking by parameter count, which is why the released pipeline budgets the search in trials and
applies no time cap by default. Apart from the right panel of Figure~\ref{fig:blockpos}, which uses three of its adapters to show
where in a block adaptation gains, none of those adapters produces a number in the paper; the
reported results use the hand-picked configuration of Phase~2.

\paragraph{Membership screens.} The news corpus additionally passes three reference-free membership screens
per model---Min-K++ (normalised likelihood of the lowest-probability $K{=}20\%$ target tokens),
context sensitivity (target NLL with and without sampled same-corpus prefixes), and, for flagged
candidates only, preference over two word-swap/placeholder perturbations. The global union
quarantined $80$ unique sequences, after which every model receives the identical retained corpus.
Near-$100\%$ preference for the unperturbed original is expected---fluency alone produces it---so we
draw no memorisation conclusion from it (\texttt{results/contamination/}).

\paragraph{The cold start, seen a second way.} Varying the inference context on $64$ fixed held-out
target windows from $16$ to $2048$ tokens reproduces Figure~\ref{fig:blockpos}'s effect
non-positionally: both models improve with context and the larger improves faster, so any protocol
that scores short contexts under-rates the models that use long ones. Measured context sensitivity is
$0.032$ bits per byte per doubling, which is the constant the block-alignment bound below uses.

\paragraph{Token-aligned blocks: a real bias, bounded.} Evaluation blocks are 512 \emph{tokens}
rather than a fixed byte span, so models with different tokenizers score slightly different amounts
of text---5.92--6.24\,MB across the eleven-model cohort, a $5\%$ spread, and $9.9\%$ across the
seventeen. Bounding the resulting bias with the measured context sensitivity puts it at
$0.08\times$ the smallest adjacent rank gap in the eleven-model cohort. In the extended cohort the
bound is looser and worth stating: on the worst adjacent pair, Qwen2.5-3B over Falcon3-7B, whose BPB
gap is only $0.29\%$, the bound reaches $0.61\times$ the gap. It still \textbf{overturns no rank},
but byte-aligned blocking would remove the question, and we recommend it for future work.

\paragraph{Seed variation is negligible.} Every adaptation cell ran with the \texttt{torch} RNG
unseeded, so a preregistered check re-ran all $17$ news models three times ($51$ cells, everything
else pinned). Median within-model SD of adapted BPB is $0.000087$ against a between-model SD of
$0.074340$, a ratio of $0.0012$, and \emph{no} pair of models within $2\times$ in size---$0$ of
$40$---reverses its ordering across seeds. The spread is not uniform: Gemma-4-12B is the one model where seed noise is
visible at all (SD $0.00315$, $5.3\times$ the next-largest spread).

\paragraph{Steering: direction, not magnitude.} Adapting on general text raises agreement with
HellaSwag on news from $\rho = 0.718$ to $0.982$ while GSM8K falls from $0.800$ to $0.700$ and
MMLU-Pro from $0.845$ to $0.773$; adapting on contamination-screened arXiv mathematics instead
raises GSM8K agreement to $0.909$ and restores the GSM8K oracle pick at zero regret,
while three general-text corpora tried as controls all leave GSM8K at or below the do-nothing
baseline. That is the rotation, and it is why the body reports only its direction. Because the
steering hypothesis predicts a
\emph{joint} movement, the better-powered statistic is a difference in differences---(GSM8K minus
HellaSwag selection accuracy under mathematics adaptation) minus the same quantity under a
general-text corpus---which is $+0.242$ pooled. Resampling models gives a strictly positive
difference in $93.7\%$ of draws; the stricter publisher-family clustering the mechanism itself
implies gives $80.9\%$ with not one negative draw in $20{,}000$, but no longer clears $0.05$ on any
tie convention. A leave-one-family-out jackknife shows why: dropping the two Gemma models takes the
pooled difference from $+0.242$ to $+0.056$. The remedy is more \emph{families}, not more models.

\paragraph{Three reproduction controls.} Re-running two Ministral-3-14B cells weeks later on
different hardware reproduced zero-shot BPB exactly and adapted BPB to within $0.0003$, two orders of
magnitude below the differences compared. Because the extension models were adapted on a different
software stack, a published cell (Qwen2.5-7B) was re-run through the extension pipeline; the analysis
refuses to pool the cohorts unless it reproduces within $1\%$, and it reproduces to $0.01\%$ on
adapted BPB ($0.7169$ against $0.7170$). And because benchmark rankings are themselves estimates,
recomputing selection accuracy while discarding pairs whose benchmark gap is smaller than $k$
combined standard errors leaves the result intact and slightly improves it: on GSM8K $0.909$ at
$k{=}0$ becomes $0.933$ at $k{=}3$, and on HellaSwag $0.836$ becomes $0.863$, so the metric's errors
concentrate in pairs the benchmark itself cannot separate.

Section~\ref{sec:divergence} reports the result; this is how it was built, and what it does and
does not license. It ran in two stages, each preregistered in code before its job returned: first the
two pairs on which the selectors disagree most (Gemma-4-12B against Ministral-3-14B, Llama-3.2-1B
against Qwen-2.5-1.5B), then every cohort model that fits one GPU, so that no pair is chosen by
anyone. Gemma-4-31B and Qwen-3.5-35B-MoE need more than one GPU \emph{for this fine-tune}---its
$1{,}280$-token sequences and its generation pass cost far more memory than the $512$-token
adaptation priced in Section~\ref{sec:intro}---and are excluded by that rule, which costs one in-band
pair of forty.

\paragraph{The task.} Journalistic convention makes a news article's first paragraph a standalone
summary of it, so the corpus supplies its own supervision: the input is the article with its opening
paragraph removed, truncated to $3{,}000$ characters, and the target is that paragraph. Articles
qualify if they have at least three paragraphs, an opening paragraph of $200$--$700$ characters
ending in sentence punctuation, and at least $800$ characters of remaining body. Of the $11{,}905$
validation documents, $3{,}627$ qualify; $2{,}000$ are sampled for training and $500$ for evaluation
under a fixed seed.

\paragraph{Why the validation split.} The BPB test blocks live in the \emph{test} split, so the
fine-tuning task is built from the validation split instead. That is verified rather than assumed: the
test split reproduces from an independent reconstruction, and the adaptation pipeline's contamination pass---which would have split the corpus differently---is off by
default and recorded as off in every published cell. Evaluation documents are then hashed against the
test documents twice, on full normalised text and on a $400$-character opening, the second because a
syndicated wire story is republished with an edited body and an identical lede. Four articles were
dropped by that second check and none by the first.

\paragraph{Equal preparation.} Both members of a pair get the paper's own adaptation recipe unchanged
---LoRA rank $16$, $\alpha{=}32$, dropout $0.05$, learning rate $10^{-4}$, effective batch $32$, $250$
steps---with prompt tokens masked out of the loss so the model is trained to write the lede rather
than to reconstruct the article. Nothing is tuned on either model, because a per-model search is
exactly the cost this protocol exists to avoid. The tokenizers differ, so the budget is matched in
update steps $\times$ batch $\times$ sequence length; each model's realised token count is in the
artifact. Decoding is greedy with a $180$-token cap and each checkpoint's own generation
configuration otherwise untouched, following Appendix~\ref{app:rescore}. The prompt is assembled
so that the instruction always survives truncation: the \emph{body} is cut to fit, never the
instruction after it, because right-truncating the assembled prompt would delete the instruction from
the longest articles at a different article count for each tokenizer---an asymmetry between the two
models being compared. At most four of the $500$ evaluation articles were cut for any model, and
none for three of them.

\paragraph{The decision rules, fixed before each run.} For the two chosen pairs: mean ROUGE-L F1
over the $500$ held-out articles, a paired bootstrap over articles ($20{,}000$ draws, $95\%$ percentile
interval) and a practical-equivalence margin of $0.01$---an interval excluding zero resolves the pair
for whichever reading predicted that winner. The first pair resolved for adapted BPB; the second was
inconclusive. For the full cohort: the fine-tuned ROUGE-L is the outcome and every selector is scored with the paper's own statistic---pairwise accuracy within $2\times$, model-level
cluster bootstrap, $20{,}000$ draws (Table~\ref{tab:downstream}). A separate guard voids any
comparison in which fine-tuning failed to improve a model over its own base weights, since ordering
unmoved models would order their base behaviour under another name. It did not fire: all fifteen
models gained between $+0.041$ and $+0.153$.

\paragraph{A second recipe.} Both readers of an earlier draft raised the same objection: the
fine-tune reused the selector's own recipe, so a model that adapts quickly under it could win both.
Recipe B changes every setting the two shared---LoRA rank $64$, $\alpha{=}128$, learning rate
$3\times10^{-4}$, effective batch $16$, $400$ steps, a new seed---on the same task, data, decoding and
metric, preregistered before it ran. It fits the training articles far harder and scores slightly
below recipe A for every model, yet orders the fifteen systems almost identically (Spearman $0.982$).
Against recipe-B systems adapted BPB remains the top selector at $0.923$ $[0.750, 1.000]$, ahead of
HellaSwag at $0.846$, parameter count at $0.744$ and GSM8K and MMLU-Pro near chance, and it picks the
better system on all $20$ pairs recipe B resolves. Its margin over parameter count, resolved under
recipe A, does not resolve here ($+0.179$ $[-0.036, +0.441]$), so that margin should be read as
recipe-specific (Table~\ref{tab:downstream}).

\paragraph{What copying buys.} A news body restates its own lede, so reference overlap is partly
available by retrieval. Two reference points bound it: the article's first body sentence scores
$0.125$ against the reference and a random body sentence $0.111$. Unadapted base weights land close
to copying---inspection confirms they largely do copy---while every fine-tuned system clears it,
from $0.188$ (Qwen-2.5-0.5B) to $0.249$ (Gemma-4-12B).

\section{The Benchmark Column Re-Score}
\label{app:rescore}
\paragraph{Harness configuration.} MMLU-Pro is 5-shot chain-of-thought with exact match on one
stratified $1{,}000$-question subset (seed 42); HellaSwag is 10-shot length-normalised
multiple-choice accuracy on all $10{,}042$ validation examples; GSM8K is 5-shot chain-of-thought with
flexible numeric extraction on all $1{,}319$ test examples. All bf16, greedy, tokenizer-native BOS.

July's eleven-model column was scored on a host that no longer exists and generative scores do not
pool across hosts---identical released weights move $-0.60$ to $+0.69$ GSM8K points when only the
host changes, A100 to H100---so it was re-scored in full---seventeen models $\times$ three benchmarks $=$
fifty-one cells, one host (A100-SXM-64GB), one build---with the design and the exclusion rule fixed
in advance. Task definitions match exactly ($17/17$ identical task hashes on both hashed benchmarks)
and every overlapping cell agrees with July to within $1.3$ points, most to within $0.3$.

Reaching that took one correction worth reporting, because the fault was in our own fix. Because the
newer backend honours each checkpoint's shipped \texttt{generation\_config.json} and the original did
not, we replaced every checkpoint's generation settings with one identical policy---which also
discarded \texttt{suppress\_tokens}, a setting that is live for gemma-4-12B. That single omission cost
it $9.86$ GSM8K and $14.30$ MMLU-Pro points while leaving HellaSwag, scored by likelihood and never
calling \texttt{generate()}, intact. Keeping the setting for all seventeen models reproduces July to
$+1.21$ points on GSM8K, $+0.10$ on MMLU-Pro and $-0.27$ on HellaSwag. That GSM8K cell is the one
outside the preregistered $1.0$-point tolerance, within the allowance of one that the re-score
declared in advance: the column is within its stated allowance rather than clean, and those are
different claims. The lesson generalises: a wrapper imposed to remove a confound can be the confound, so
the unmodified harness belongs in the design as a control.

\begin{abstract}
Fixed task benchmarks expose important capabilities, but their model rankings can conflict and their
accuracy scores discard most token-level evidence. Predictive cross-entropy uses every target token,
yet ordinary perplexity cannot compare models with different tokenizers. We turn the training
objective into a practical base-model selection protocol: normalize held-out loss by UTF-8 bytes,
refresh the target-domain corpus, conservatively quarantine contamination-risk candidates, and
measure every model before and after the same controlled adaptation procedure. We apply the protocol
to 11 base models (0.5B--35B parameters) on news, Reddit, and Hacker News. On the marker-masked news
evaluation, adapted BPB and HellaSwag have near-identical rankings (Spearman $\rho=0.982$, seven of
11 exact ranks, maximum displacement one), as expected for two conditional-continuation measures.
MMLU-Pro and GSM8K agree less ($\rho=0.764$ and $0.727$): Qwen models move upward, while Gemma
models move downward, exposing family-specific knowledge, arithmetic, prompting, and answer-format
effects that general text likelihood should not erase. Within each corpus, adaptation materially
changes the zero-shot ordering ($\rho=0.609$--$0.682$); after adaptation, rankings transfer strongly
across corpora ($\rho=0.991$, $0.955$, and $0.964$). Diagnostic token- and context-level studies show
that adaptation largely corrects surface-form calibration and retains its gain through 2{,}048-token
inference. The release provides code, a versioned protocol, dataset fingerprints, auditable results,
and a submission-ready leaderboard.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
For decades, language models were compared by predictive cross-entropy and its exponentiated form,
perplexity. The metric was broad and statistically efficient---every next token contributed
evidence---but token-level normalization made scores incomparable across vocabularies. Evaluation
then shifted toward fixed task suites. MMLU-Pro, HellaSwag, and GSM8K are useful precisely because
they isolate different capabilities \cite{wang2024mmlupro,zellers2019hellaswag,cobbe2021gsm8k}; the
same specialization also makes their rankings disagree. Once benchmark questions become development
targets or enter pre-training corpora, a high score may additionally reflect task preparation or
overlap rather than transferable base-model quality \cite{xu2024survey,zhang2025trainbeforetest}.

Recent evidence makes the return to loss timely. Cross-entropy can have higher signal and lower noise
than accuracy \cite{heineman2025signal}, and train-before-test evaluations show that light,
task-specific preparation substantially harmonizes benchmark rankings and restores their connection
to perplexity \cite{zhang2025trainbeforetest}. The unresolved practical question is how to make that
connection useful when choosing among off-the-shelf base models with different tokenizers for a new
domain.

We answer with a controlled \emph{adapt-then-measure} protocol on evaluator-owned text. Bits per byte
(BPB) places every model on one physical denominator. A recent target-domain snapshot limits
staleness; internal deduplication and model-based risk signals trigger conservative quarantine before
training or scoring; and a fixed LoRA configuration measures candidates under the same data and
update budget. The endpoint, adapted BPB, answers ``which base model best models this domain after
equal preparation?'' The reduction from zero-shot to adapted BPB answers a different question:
``how much calibration did this corpus require?'' Neither number is presented as a substitute for a
task benchmark when the deployment task is already known.

Our contributions are: (1) a tokenizer-agnostic, fresh-data protocol for comparing adaptable base
models; (2) a complete 11-model accuracy and rank audit on MMLU-Pro, HellaSwag, and GSM8K that
explains where entropy rankings agree and diverge; (3) a 33-cell, three-domain experiment separating
within-domain adaptation effects from cross-domain rank transfer; (4) token- and context-level
diagnostics of what the adapters learn; and (5) a public implementation with versioned benchmark
manifests, dataset fingerprints, machine-readable results, and a validated leaderboard. Code and
artifacts are available at \url{https://github.com/DavidAfonsoValente/entropy-bench}.

\section{Related Work}

\textbf{From perplexity to reliable loss evaluation.} Language modeling was originally grounded in
byte- and character-level entropy \cite{shannon1951prediction}, while token-level perplexity depends
on each model's vocabulary and segmentation \cite{mielke2019perplexity,ahia2023languages}.
Normalizing log-likelihood by UTF-8 bytes restores a common unit: The Pile used BPB for
cross-tokenizer comparison \cite{gao2020pile}, and Paloma standardized broad, domain-resolved
measurement of language-model fit \cite{magnusson2023paloma}. Heineman et al. evaluate 465 models
over 30 benchmarks and show that loss-based metrics can offer better signal, noise, and scaling-law
reliability than accuracy \cite{heineman2025signal}. Our contribution is therefore not BPB itself;
it is the operational protocol around the metric for comparing heterogeneous, off-the-shelf base
models on new target data.

\textbf{Why task rankings disagree.} MMLU-Pro, HellaSwag, and GSM8K measure knowledge, conditional
continuation, and mathematical problem solving under distinct elicitation and scoring rules
\cite{wang2024mmlupro,zellers2019hellaswag,cobbe2021gsm8k}. Such scores can be indispensable for a
known deployment task, but accuracy is discrete and benchmark-specific. Observational scaling work
finds that a low-dimensional capability structure explains much public-model performance
\cite{ruan2024observational}, while controlled model families exhibit a smooth relationship between
validation loss and average downstream error \cite{gadre2025scales}. We test that relationship across
families and report both alignment and systematic residuals rather than collapsing the tasks into an
uninterpreted macro-average.

\textbf{Equal preparation.} Dynamic evaluation updates a language model on recent test
history to improve predictive likelihood \cite{krause2018dynamic,krause2019dynamictransformer};
domain-adaptive pre-training improves fit to a target distribution \cite{gururangan2020dapt}.
Closest to our model-selection motivation, Zhang et al.'s \emph{train-before-test} gives 61 models
identical supervised, benchmark-specific preparation across 24 tasks: average cross-benchmark
Kendall agreement rises from $0.52$ to $0.76$, and pre-adaptation BPB predicts post-adaptation task
potential for base models \cite{zhang2025trainbeforetest}. Their result motivates our design but the
estimand differs. They rank post-fine-tuning task accuracy on public labeled benchmarks; we rank
held-out language-model fit after unsupervised adaptation to a fresh target-domain corpus. We also
standardize the byte denominator, screen contamination risk, and test rank transfer across raw-text
domains.

\textbf{Contamination controls.} Models can memorize duplicated pre-training text
\cite{carlini2021extracting,carlini2022quantifying}; deduplication and contamination analysis are
therefore necessary \cite{lee2022deduplicating,xu2024survey}. Reference-free membership signals such
as Min-K\% and Min-K++ are useful screens \cite{shi2023mink,zhang2024minkpp}, but no black-box score
proves that a sample occurred in a proprietary training set. We consequently use soft signals only
for conservative quarantine and report them as risk indicators, not verified leaks.

\section{The Evaluation Pipeline}
\label{sec:pipeline}
The pipeline (Figure~\ref{fig:pipeline}) is chronological: corpus construction and screening are
completed before any sample can influence adaptation or scoring.

\subsection{Phase 1: Deduplication and Contamination-Risk Screening}
We first enforce exact split disjointness and remove exact and MinHash/LSH near-duplicates. For
models whose pre-training corpora are unavailable, we then apply three reference-free screens:
\begin{itemize}[noitemsep,topsep=0pt]
    \item \textbf{Min-K++:} average the normalized likelihood of the lowest-probability $K=20\%$
    target tokens, using the model's probability-weighted vocabulary mean and variance at each
    position; unusually high within-split outliers become candidates.
    \item \textbf{Context sensitivity:} compare target NLL with and without randomly sampled
    same-corpus prefix documents; unusually large confidence gains add a soft risk signal.
    \item \textbf{Perturbation preference:} for already flagged candidates, compare the original
    sequence with two word-swap/placeholder perturbations. Preference for the original triggers
    conservative quarantine, but is not treated as proof of memorization because fluency alone can
    produce the same outcome.
\end{itemize}
The screen runs per model and the union of candidate indices is quarantined globally. Thus every
model receives the same retained corpus. This policy reduces overlap risk without making an
unverifiable claim about membership in proprietary pre-training data.

\subsection{Phase 2: Controlled Domain Adaptation}
The retained corpus is packed into contiguous $L{=}512$-token blocks along document boundaries.
Every headline model--corpus cell uses LoRA rank 16, $\alpha=32$, dropout $0.05$, learning rate
$10^{-4}$, effective batch 32, \texttt{all-linear} targets, and 250 update steps. This fixes the data
and update budget, although larger models still require more FLOPs and have more trainable LoRA
parameters. Separately, the mechanistic diagnostics in Section~\ref{sec:tokengain} use three adapters
selected by an exploratory multi-fidelity sweep; those runs explain the source of adaptation gains
but do not determine the public ranking.

\subsection{Phase 3: Tokenizer-Agnostic BPB Evaluation}
Cross-entropy loss ($\loss_{\text{nats}}$) aggregated over the held-out corpus is unified onto byte-space using the UTF-8 byte-length ($|B|$) of the source text:
\begin{equation}
\bpb = \frac{\loss_{\text{nats}}}{\ln(2)} \times \frac{|Tokens|}{|B|}
\label{eq:bpb}
\end{equation}
Zero-shot and adapted BPB are both measured on the held-out test split. Loss is accumulated over
scored tokens and divided by the exact UTF-8 byte count represented by those targets. The packed test
stream uses independent, non-overlapping 512-token blocks, so context resets at every cut. Injected
document-start markers remain available as context but are always masked as prediction targets in
the headline protocol. This point matters: an older exploratory run scored those markers; all core
tables, correlations, the manifest, and the leaderboard use the corrected marker-masked rerun.

\section{Experimental Setup}
\paragraph{Models.} We evaluate 11 base checkpoints spanning dense Transformers, one sparse MoE,
and one Liquid architecture (0.5B--35B parameters); the cohort is listed in
Tables~\ref{tab:domain_transfer} and~\ref{tab:bpb_static}.

\paragraph{Data.} The primary snapshot is Google News dated 8 June 2026 (119{,}054 articles,
$\sim$361\,MB of text). Reddit and Hacker News provide the transfer check in
Table~\ref{tab:corpora}. Every corpus uses an 80/10/10 train/validation/test split (seed 42) with
zero ID and normalized-text intersection across splits.

\paragraph{Static benchmarks.} For Section~\ref{sec:corr}, every base model is scored with
\emph{lm-evaluation-harness} 0.4.12 and vLLM 0.25.1 (bf16, greedy generation, tokenizer-native BOS).
MMLU-Pro uses 5-shot chain-of-thought and exact match on one stratified 1{,}000-question subset
(seed 42); HellaSwag uses 10-shot length-normalized multiple-choice accuracy on all 10{,}042
validation examples; GSM8K uses 5-shot chain-of-thought and flexible numeric extraction on all
1{,}319 test examples. These are directly observed scores under one harness, not vendor-reported
model-card values.

\paragraph{Descriptive uncertainty and ranks.} We orient every rank so 1 is best, compute Pearson
correlation on score values, Spearman correlation on ranks, and Kendall agreement on pairwise order.
Wilson 95\% intervals show test-set sampling uncertainty for accuracy. They do not include
run-to-run, prompt, subset-selection, or model-family uncertainty; the cohort is purposive, so all
correlations are descriptive rather than population estimates.

\paragraph{Hardware.} Adaptation ran under Slurm on the Leonardo (CINECA) GPU partition, 3 nodes $\times$ 4 NVIDIA A100 64\,GB; the static-benchmark suite ran separately on A100 GPUs.

\section{Results and Discussion}

\subsection{Core Result: Adapted Rankings Transfer Across Domains}
\label{sec:domains}

does the ordering identify broadly strong base models rather than one news-specific winner? We
prepare three corpora with the same grouping and cleaning recipe---exact deduplication, Gopher
quality heuristics, and MinHash/LSH near-duplicate filtering at Jaccard $0.60$---and run all 11
models on every corpus.

\paragraph{Adaptation changes the choice.} Zero-shot and adapted rankings are only moderately
aligned: Spearman $\rho=0.655$ on news, $0.609$ on Reddit, and $0.682$ on Hacker News, with maximum
movements of seven, seven, and five places. Gemma-4-31B moves from fourth, fifth, and sixth
zero-shot to first adapted; Gemma-4-12B moves from ninth in every zero-shot ranking to second,
second, and fourth. Adapted BPB is therefore not a rescaling of ordinary held-out loss; it estimates
fit after equal target-domain preparation.

\paragraph{The adapted order transfers.} News and Reddit agree on nine of eleven exact ranks
(Spearman $\rho=0.991$; maximum displacement one). News versus Hacker News yields $\rho=0.955$ and
Reddit versus Hacker News $\rho=0.964$, with maximum displacement two. Gemma-4-31B ranks first on
all three corpora. Figure~\ref{fig:crosscorpus} shows that the result is not only ordinal: Pearson
score correlations are $0.992$, $0.977$, and $0.990$.

\paragraph{Rank changes are near-tie changes.} The three-model mid-size tier spans $0.0259$ BPB on
news but only $0.0089$ on Hacker News; the two-model small tier spans $0.0258$ and $0.0088$.
On Hacker News, Ministral-3-14B ($0.835$), Qwen-3.5-35B-MoE ($0.836$), and Gemma-4-12B
($0.844$) are separated by $0.009$ BPB. A target-domain run should resolve choices inside such a
tier; the cross-domain result supports the broader tier ordering, not an immutable total order.

\paragraph{Difficulty and order are distinct.} Every model has its lowest BPB on news and highest
on Reddit. Gemma-4-31B moves from $0.624$ on news to $0.913$ on Reddit, a $46\%$ increase, yet
remains first. Absolute BPB describes domain difficulty and attainable fit; relative rank describes
which candidate is strongest under the shared adaptation protocol.

\paragraph{Surface-form calibration.} Gemma-4-12B's largest movers are
\texttt{<bos>}, punctuation, paragraph breaks, and common function words; LFM-2.5 shows the same
pattern. Across the top 200 gaining tokens, formatting, punctuation, and function words account for
$83\%$ of the gain for both models, while content words account for $5.2\%$ for Gemma and $14.0\%$
for LFM. Qwen-2.5-1.5B begins closer to the corpus distribution and allocates more of its smaller
gain to dates and figures. For Gemma, the marker and its next 16 tokens contribute
$3.6\%+5.0\%=8.6\%$ of this exploratory gain. These observations explain the three selected
adapters; they are not assumed to identify a universal mechanism for all 11 models.

\paragraph{Context reveals steady-state quality.} Figure~\ref{fig:blockpos} resolves loss and gain by position in the 512-token block and normalizes every bucket to UTF-8 bytes. Over the first eight positions, Gemma-4-12B averages $2.566$ bits/byte and Qwen-2.5-1.5B averages $1.490$; by positions $256$--$510$, Gemma reaches $0.717$ and Qwen $0.773$. The crossing therefore remains after byte normalization and is not explained by vocabulary size alone. Once context is available, the 12B model shows its stronger steady-state language modelling. The opening 32 positions account for $23.0\%$ of Gemma's total gain and $12.9\%$ of Qwen's, making context recovery a clear component of the calibration signal.

\paragraph{The 512-token adaptation transfers to longer inference contexts.} We select 64 fixed held-out target windows and vary only the preceding context from 16 to 2{,}048 tokens, scoring the same target tokens at every length with exact byte normalization. Figure~\ref{fig:contextlength} shows that Gemma's base BPB continues to recover beyond the training window, from $0.6465$ at 512 tokens to $0.6054$ at 2{,}048. Its adapted BPB is effectively unchanged ($0.5473\rightarrow0.5474$), so the adapter trained on 512-token blocks retains a $9.6\%$ advantage at four times that context. Qwen's smaller adaptation advantage also remains positive at 2{,}048 tokens ($0.7400\rightarrow0.7271$, a $1.7\%$ reduction). Thus the base model continues to benefit from longer context, especially for Gemma, while the learned adaptation remains stable outside its training length.

\paragraph{Interpretation.} The adapted BPB level remains the selection metric. The
zero-shot$\rightarrow$adapted reduction is a calibration diagnostic; it must not be ranked as if a
larger change implied a better endpoint.

\paragraph{HellaSwag and BPB measure the same local object.} HellaSwag selects a continuation by
length-normalized conditional log-likelihood, whereas BPB aggregates conditional log-likelihood over
every target token. Their rank agreement is consequently $0.982$: seven models keep their exact
rank and none moves more than one place. Gemma-4-31B leads both (adapted BPB $0.624$, HellaSwag
$87.9\%$). This is convergent validity for conditional language modeling, not a claim that either
metric measures every capability.

\paragraph{Knowledge and elicitation.} MMLU-Pro agreement remains substantial but
weaker ($\rho=0.764$). The Qwen-3.5-35B-MoE leads at $62.0\%$ and moves from BPB rank 3 to MMLU rank
1; Qwen-3.5-9B moves from 5 to 3. Gemma-4-12B moves from BPB rank 2 to MMLU rank 7. Unlike
HellaSwag's likelihood comparison, our MMLU-Pro protocol generates 5-shot chains of thought and
requires an extractable exact answer. The residual therefore mixes factual coverage, reasoning,
few-shot pattern continuation, and answer formatting.

\paragraph{GSM8K exposes a family-specific residual.} No model keeps the same rank. Every Qwen
model is unchanged or improves relative to BPB; both Gemma models decline, with Gemma-4-12B moving
from rank 2 to 7. The contrast is large even among small models: Qwen-2.5-1.5B reaches $61.1\%$
GSM8K at BPB rank 9, while Llama-3.2-1B reaches $6.5\%$ at BPB rank 8. This supports a narrow
interpretation: arithmetic data, chain-of-thought preparation, and generation-format compatibility
vary systematically by model family. Because architecture, data mixture, token budget, and release
generation are confounded, the experiment does not identify which cause dominates.

\paragraph{Point-estimate leaders are not equally resolved.} HellaSwag's leader exceeds the runner-up
by $2.41$ percentage points (independent-binomial $z=5.03$), and their Wilson intervals do not
overlap. MMLU-Pro's $4.2$-point top gap has $z=1.92$ and overlapping intervals; GSM8K's top two Qwen
models differ by only $0.53$ points ($z=0.40$). Boldface therefore identifies the observed maximum,
not a universal or necessarily significant winner. The repo publishes the numerators, denominators,
intervals, ranks, and derivation script in \texttt{results/static\_benchmark\_analysis.json}.

\section{Reporting Standard and Limitations}
\label{sec:reporting}
The evidence supports a practical reporting contract and also fixes the boundary of the claim.

\paragraph{Version the full estimand.} A leaderboard row is comparable only when corpus revision,
split, context length, marker masking, risk screen, adaptation configuration, update budget, and BPB
aggregation match. Any change creates a new benchmark ID. The public leaderboard validates these
fields rather than accepting an isolated number.

\paragraph{Separate level, change, and uncertainty.} Adapted BPB ranks endpoint fit;
zero-shot$\rightarrow$adapted reduction describes calibration. Task accuracies retain their own
denominators and intervals. A large reduction, bold point estimate, or tiny numerical BPB gap should
not be promoted into a stronger claim than the underlying comparison supports.

\paragraph{Scope of model selection.} Adapted BPB measures base-model potential after the specified
unsupervised domain intervention. Zero-shot BPB better describes immediate language-model fit, and a
task benchmark remains the relevant criterion when the deployment task and elicitation protocol are
known. The framework complements rather than replaces capability and safety evaluation.

\paragraph{Limitations.} The cohort contains only 11 English-centric base models and confounds
architecture, data mixture, token budget, release generation, and parameter count. Fixed update and
data budgets are not equal-FLOP budgets. Accuracy intervals cover test-set sampling only; static
scores can change with prompts, harness versions, and extraction. The three diagnostic adapters and
64-window context study are explanatory probes, not cohort-wide replications. Reference-free
contamination signals justify conservative removal but cannot verify proprietary training
membership. Finally, the news text is third-party material: the release publishes its exact
record-level fingerprint and verification tooling, but not the raw text without redistribution
permission. These constraints limit causal and universal claims while leaving every reported
comparison auditable.

\section{Conclusion}
The old choice between incomparable perplexities and narrow task accuracies is unnecessary. A recent
raw-text snapshot, a byte denominator, conservative risk screening, and a fixed adaptation protocol
turn predictive loss into an auditable base-model selection experiment. In our 11-model study,
adaptation changes which models look strongest, yet the adapted order transfers across three domains.

The benchmark residuals make the result more useful, not less. HellaSwag almost reproduces the BPB
order because both measure conditional continuation. MMLU-Pro and GSM8K reorder families because
they additionally require knowledge, arithmetic, few-shot elicitation, generation, and answer
extraction. The resulting recommendation is two-layered: use adapted BPB on fresh target data to
choose broad base-model potential and stable tiers; use deployment-matched task and safety tests to
resolve capability requirements and close calls.

Relative to train-before-test, the distinction is labels and estimand: it harmonizes ranks after
supervised task adaptation; we compare off-the-shelf bases after unlabeled target-text preparation,
across tokenizers. Released protocol checks make each leaderboard value traceable.
